1 · What Happened

The team allocation moved for the second time, and it used the same mechanism as June and July: the contract's code was replaced first. At 15:29:13 UTC on 21 August 2026 the Gnosis Safe upgraded Team Pool 2's implementation. Twenty-two seconds later, at 15:29:35, it called a function that did not exist in the previous code and pushed 2,567.8333mn AKE ($21,593,062) to fourteen addresses that had never held a token, never sent a transaction, and hold no BNB to this day. None of the other seven pools was touched.

2 · Against the Published Vesting Schedule

Early Contributors have now taken 4.568bn against 2.143bn vested — 2.13× entitlement. Akedo's whitepaper gives this allocation a 6-month cliff and then linear release over 42 months. The cliff expired 21 February 2026, so exactly six of the forty-two months had elapsed on 21 August 2026.

In the main report this allocation was the one that looked compliant — 2.000bn withdrawn against 1.995bn vested, a ratio of 1.00×. That is no longer true. The 21 August release moves Early Contributors from the cleanest line in the vesting table to the second-worst after Advisors.

The counter-argument, stated fairly. The fourteen amounts are not round numbers — 787.5mn, 495.0mn, 266.6667mn, 160.7143mn, 68.4524mn — and several are exact thirds or sevenths. That is the arithmetic of per-beneficiary vesting schedules being computed and paid out, not of an arbitrary raid. It is entirely possible this is a genuine distribution to fourteen team members under terms that differ from the published ones. What the chain shows is that the total exceeds the published schedule by 2.425bn, and that the contract had to be rewritten before it could pay.

5 · Method, and What This Does Not Establish

How this was produced

  • Timestamp grid extended to the new head and validated against three freshly read blocks — maximum drift 1 second — before any date was assigned.
  • CoinGecko hourly price series refreshed through 21 Aug 16:46 UTC before anything was valued; the previous series ended 20 Aug and would have mispriced the payout.
  • Every Transfer touching the eight pools across blocks 117,047,857 – 117,266,413, topic-filtered on both sender and recipient position.
  • ERC1967 implementation slot read at both ends for all eight pools, then binary-searched to the exact upgrade block for the one that changed.
  • Function selectors extracted from deployed bytecode of both implementations and diffed; the payout calldata decoded directly and checked against the fourteen Transfer logs.
  • Safe owners and threshold, pool owner(), and every recipient's balance, nonce, BNB and code read live at head.

What is not claimed

  • Not that this is theft or a raid. The Safe is the pool's legitimate owner and was entitled to upgrade it. The amounts look like computed vesting entitlements.
  • Not that the recipients are insiders. They are unlabelled and unconnected to every set tested. They may simply be team members' fresh wallets.
  • Not who controls the Safe. Four owner addresses are known; no identity attaches to any of them.
  • Not that the excess is a breach of contract. A whitepaper schedule is a statement of intent; the comparison shows published terms against on-chain behaviour, nothing more.
  • Recipient clustering is impossible for now — with zero BNB there is no first-gas transaction to trace. This is the open question, not a finding.
What to watch next. Three things would change the reading materially: (1) whoever funds these fourteen wallets with BNB — that funder is the first real clustering signal and would immediately show whether they are independent or one operator; (2) whether any of the 2.568bn reaches an exchange, which under this analysis's rules converts a hold into a sale; and (3) whether the new implementation is reverted, as the June one was, or left in place with the batch-payout function live for future releases.
