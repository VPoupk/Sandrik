The single finding this page adds up to. Across the release days that can be followed wallet by wallet — 18.4923bn net — 2.0000bn (10.8%) has been sold, all of it the 19 June release, which reached Gate.io custody four hops and thirty-four days later. Everything else is still sitting in the wallets it was paid to. The 22 July node unlock says the same from the other end: 20.8671bn of the 20.8671bn released is in the 98 wallets it was consolidated into within five weeks. So the distributed supply has overwhelmingly not been sold — it has been concentrated, which is a different risk rather than an absent one.
This corrects an earlier version of this page, which said none of it had been sold. That was an artefact of following each recipient only one transfer forward. The 19 June recipient passed the tokens to a second wallet the next morning, that wallet to a third four weeks later, and the third deposited the lot at Gate.io — none of which a one-hop test can see. Every release is now followed until it reaches an exchange, a DEX pool, or a wallet that still holds it.

12 · Method, and what this does not show

How the numbers here are produced

  • Chain only. eth_getLogs, eth_call, eth_getStorageAt, eth_getCode and eth_getTransactionByHash against a BSC archive node. Prices come from CoinGecko; nothing else is taken from a third party.
  • Pool flow is complete, not sampled. Every AKE transfer with a pool on either side has been recorded from the token's deployment to block 123,747,689, de-duplicated on (block, logIndex), and the segments are checked to tile the chain with no gap before anything is written.
  • Hourly pricing, and its limit. Each transfer is valued at the CoinGecko hourly AKE/USD rate interpolated to its own block timestamp — the right mark for a transfer at a moment. Price ranges use OHLC candles instead, because an hourly series missed the 20 September top by 55%. Both are cross-checked in §9.
  • The sale test. A transfer into any exchange except Binance counts as a sale. Anything still in a wallet is a hold, no matter how many wallets it is spread across. Binance flows are reported separately because deposits there are routinely custody and market-making moves.
  • Follow-through is a balance, not an inference. "Still holding" is balanceOf at the head block for each recipient, and "reached an exchange" is the sum of that wallet's own onward transfers into a labelled custody address.

What this does not establish

  • Who controls the recipient wallets. Fresh addresses receiving a scheduled distribution from a team-controlled pool is what the chain shows. Whether they are employees, investors, market makers or the team itself is not on-chain and is not asserted here.
  • Intent. Sequences are reported as sequences. A deposit an hour before a release is a sequence; it is not evidence of coordination.
  • Centralised order books. The great majority of reported volume happens on exchanges and leaves no on-chain record at all. Where this page says a move was not on-chain driven, that is a statement about what the chain can account for, not a claim to have seen the order book.
  • Off-chain entitlement. Nothing here shows what anyone was owed or agreed.
