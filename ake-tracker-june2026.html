The single largest movement in this window is not trading. On 22 July the three Nodes pools released 20,867,088,139,000,000,000,000,000,000 wei (20.867bn AKE) to 5,879 wallets. On 29 August, between 11:10 and 11:47 UTC, 20,867,088,039,000,000,000,000,000,000 wei (20.867bn AKE) arrived in 98 wallets that had never transacted before and have not moved since. The two figures differ by 100 AKE across 5,879 wallets. None of it touched an exchange.

The six things worth knowing

#FindingEvidence
1The entire 22 July node unlock has been consolidated into 98 fresh wallets. 5,879 claimant wallets → 98 hubs (~60 claimants each) → 98 never-used wallets. Completed 29 Aug. §3
2The July price run happened inside a ladder of the market maker's own sell walls. 2.79bn of single-sided AKE liquidity placed 16–23 July between $0.00105 and $0.00306; price ran from $0.00019 to $0.00306 and every wall filled. §4
3Volume was 2.6–3.9× market cap for six consecutive days. 16–19 July turnover 3.67/2.57/3.94/3.19. No genuine spot market turns its whole capitalisation four times a day.§2, §4
4On 26 August the market maker closed every position and left, taking 1,093.61 BNB and 11.58mn AKE. Active liquidity fell 13.2%; five hours later the on-chain price broke 39% in fifteen minutes.§5
5It came back 24 hours later — funded by Team Pool 2 supply. Two of the 14 recipients of the 21 August team release moved for the first time; 70.00mn of that supply now sits in the AKE/WBNB pool as liquidity.§6
6The market maker is downstream of the deployer's launch-day distribution. Deployer → 13.800bn → 11.650bn hub → 100.00mn → the wallet that has run the pool's book since 21 August 2025.§8

3 · The 22 July node unlock and where all of it went

On 22 July 2026 the three Nodes pools released 20,867,088,139,000,000,000,000,000,000 wei = 20.867bn AKE to 5,879 distinct wallets. On 29 August 2026, 11:10–11:47 UTC, 20,867,088,039,000,000,000,000,000,000 wei = 20.867bn AKE landed in 98 wallets that had never sent a transaction. The two totals differ by 100 AKE — dust across 5,879 wallets.

What this does and does not establish. It establishes that the tokens released to 5,879 node claimants on 22 July are now held in 98 places, that the routing was systematic, and that none of it reached an exchange. It does not establish who controls those 98 wallets. A single operator sweeping wallets it always controlled, a custodian consolidating client balances, and a buyer acquiring claims off-chain all produce this pattern. What can be said from the chain is that 20.867bn — 20.9% of total supply and roughly 92% of circulating supply — is under materially fewer hands than the claimant list suggests.

4 · Market-maker range orders vs the July volume spike

A Uniswap-V3-style position funded with only AKE and no BNB is a limit sell: the range sits above spot, and as price rises through it the AKE is sold for BNB. It never appears as a transfer to an exchange, so a transfer-based sales ledger misses it entirely. Scanning every Mint on the PancakeV3 AKE/WBNB 0.01% pool over the token's whole life finds 16 such positions totalling 3.82bn AKE placed.

Where that volume was not

On 25 July the token reported $220,999,738 of volume while only $4,955,908 of AKE crossed an exchange custody wallet on-chain — a ratio of 45×. On 23–24 July the ratio was under 1×, meaning on-chain settlement exceeded reported volume, which is what genuine deposit-and-withdraw activity around an unlock looks like.

Neither reading is proof on its own. Together with a sell ladder placed by one operator on the same days, they describe a market being made rather than one clearing.

Read this carefully. Placing range orders is ordinary market-making, and a project having a market maker is ordinary. Three things here are not ordinary: the walls were funded with AKE and no quote asset, so they could only ever sell; they were laid immediately before and during a 16× price move; and the operator behind them traces to the deployer's launch-day distribution (§8). The gross 3.82bn placed is an upper bound on selling, not a figure — the same tokens were withdrawn and re-placed at new ranges repeatedly. Netting the position manager against the pools gives +88.41mn AKE actually delivered to the market across both operator wallets over their whole lives; the current operator net sold 148.71mn while its predecessor net bought 71.89mn.

11 · Method, and what this does not show

How the numbers were produced

  • Chain data only. Every on-chain figure comes from eth_getLogs and eth_call against a NodeReal BSC archive node. No analytics vendor, no aggregator.
  • Wallet labels are read from OKLink, a first-party BSC explorer, separating entity tags from property tags. Where no label exists the document says so rather than guessing.
  • Prices are CoinGecko. Deposit valuations in §7 use the hourly series interpolated to the block's own timestamp. Daily figures in §2 and §9 use that day's close, which is the right granularity for a day-level table and the wrong one for a single transfer.
  • Liquidity is read from the pool's own Swap, Mint and Burn events and from slot0 / liquidity, so price and depth are the pool's, not a third party's view of it.
  • Pool balances are direct balanceOf reads at named block heights, not inferred from flow.
  • Double counting. Transfers are de-duplicated on (block, logIndex). Router legs are collapsed so a single swap routed through four contracts counts once. The 20.98bn "net disposal" figure that a naive per-wallet sum produces is not used anywhere here; §3 uses the terminal figure instead.

What this does not establish

  • Control. Common funding, shared gas dispensers and synchronised timing are strong circumstantial evidence of one operator. They are not proof, and this document does not claim it.
  • Off-chain trading. Selling on a centralised order book from inventory deposited weeks earlier leaves no footprint in the window it happens. §2's vol ÷ on-chain ratio measures that gap; it does not fill it.
  • Intent. Range orders, liquidity withdrawals and treasury-funded liquidity all have ordinary explanations. Where a reading cuts both ways the document gives both.
  • The 6.968bn branch from the 11.65bn hub has not been walked. It is the largest unresolved item on the board and larger than anything resolved here.
  • BscScan remains unreachable from this environment — four routes attempted, all blocked at the WAF. Every label used is OKLink's, and every raw fact is from the node directly.
