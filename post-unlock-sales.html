1 · Correction — the unlock started on 22 July, not 26 July, and there was no 10 July unlock

The main report's "1.90bn sold since the 26 July unlocks" was measured from the wrong date, and it understated the answer by a factor of six. The pools' big release was 22 July 2026 — 20,867.1mn in a single day — four days before the date I had been using. Measured from 22 July and priced hourly, sales are 12,168.1mn AKE / $40,060,286, not 1.90bn / $8.36M. The single largest selling day, 24 July, sits inside the four days the old window excluded.

Why hourly pricing changes the answer

AKE moved far too fast in this period for a daily average to be honest. The clearest case is the largest single deposit in the dataset: 2,000.0mn hitting Gate.io at 23 July 18:03 UTC. The hourly series prints $0.00251245 at 18:00 that day, against a daily average of $0.00170592 — the deposit landed straight into a spike that the daily mean smooths away. Valuing it daily gives $3,411,835; valuing it at the hour it actually happened gives $5,011,878, 47% more. Every figure in this document uses the hourly series, linearly interpolated to the block's own timestamp. The series has 2,161 points at exactly 3,600-second spacing with no gaps.

3 · Where the Sellers Got It

How this was resolved. Each of the 413 selling wallets was walked back up its inbound transfers — up to five hops, following the largest source at each step — until the chain reached an allocation pool, a known insider or gas-clustered wallet, Binance custody, another exchange, or a DEX pool. Two rounds of provenance scanning were needed: the sellers themselves, then the 143 wallets that funded the ones that did not resolve on the first pass. Where a chain still does not terminate, the wallet is reported as unresolved with its immediate funder named — not guessed at.

The single most important negative result: not one of the 22,063 wallets that claimed directly from a pool deposited to a non-Binance exchange in this window. The overlap between the 22,063 direct pool recipients and the 413 sellers is exactly zero. Only 11 wallets sold AKE that can be traced back to a pool claimant through an intermediary, and between them that is 67.0mn — 0.6% of the value. The 20.87bn claimed on 22 July has, on this evidence, not been sold by the people who claimed it.
Which makes the exchange-cycling figure the thing to read carefully. 73.2% of the value sold came from wallets whose AKE was withdrawn from another exchange first. That is the signature of market-making and arbitrage between venues, not of allocation holders liquidating — the same float moving between Gate.io, KuCoin, Kraken and MEXC. It inflates the gross sales number relative to genuine new supply hitting the market, and it is reported separately here for exactly that reason.

4 · The Unresolved Cluster, Resolved — TGE Buyers, and a Six-Hop Chain Back to the Investors Pool

The cluster splits two ways, and one half is the more serious finding in this document. Half of its value — 523.3mn ($1,310,946) — belongs to people who bought AKE on the open market on TGE day: eight wallets that each took 214.3–243.1mn from the PancakeSwap V3 AKE/WBNB pool on 21 August 2025 and held for eleven months. That is ordinary. The other half is not: 333.3mn ($828,876) traces back to the Investors Pool, moved there in three routine monthly unlock tranches and then pushed through six consecutive fresh wallets in twenty-four hours on 22–23 July before it was sold to Gate.io. That is a second pool-to-exchange route, and it is deliberately obscured in a way the June episode was not.

Why the walk stalled, and what broke the deadlock

The five-hop walk follows token flow. It stalled here because each of these sellers was funded by a wallet that received nothing at all inside the scan window — the AKE had arrived eleven months earlier, before the window opened, so there was no inbound edge to follow. Two additional passes fixed it: a lifetime inbound scan of the thirteen funder wallets, back to the token's deployment block; and first-gas tracing, which follows BNB rather than AKE and therefore works even when a wallet's token history is out of window.

The gas trace produced a false lead first, and it is worth stating plainly because the same pattern is used as evidence elsewhere in this analysis. All ten of the largest unresolved sellers were funded with exactly 0.0001 BNB from a single address, and eight of them within thirty blocks of each other at 17:47 UTC on 18 July 2026 — four days before the unlock. On the face of it that is a textbook coordinated cohort. It is not. The funding address 0x6596da8b…2051b0d0 carries the OKLink entity tag "Gate.io. Gas Fee_1": it is Gate.io's own wallet for subsidising gas on customer withdrawal addresses. Batch-funding at an identical amount and timestamp is exactly what an exchange's gas top-up run looks like. All ten sellers independently carry an exchange User tag, which is consistent.

This does not undermine the clusters in §9 of the main report, but it does sharpen what they rest on. Those wallets were funded from KuCoin. DepositAndWithdraw_5 — a customer deposit/withdrawal wallet, not a tagged gas-fee wallet — and, more importantly, the evidence there is behavioural: ten wallets calling userWithdraw() on freshly upgraded pool contracts within minutes of each other and taking 9.54bn. Common gas funding alone is weak evidence and is not treated as sufficient anywhere in this report.

What changed, and what did not. Digging into the cluster moved 523.3mn ($1,310,946) into open-market buyers — people who bought at launch and are entitled to sell whatever they like — and moved 333.3mn ($828,876) into the pool-derived column, where it raises pool-derived selling from 12.5% to 14.6% of value. 5 · Method and Limits

How the numbers were produced

  • Every Transfer touching any of the 28 non-Binance exchange custody addresses, blocks 105,042,323 → 117,047,856, topic-filtered on both sender and recipient position and de-duplicated on (block, logIndex). 23,855 rows; 6,393 are external deposits.
  • Only deposits arriving from outside the venue set count. Exchange-to-exchange custody rotation is excluded.
  • Prices: CoinGecko hourly series for akedo, 2,161 points at exact 3,600s spacing, linearly interpolated to each deposit block's own timestamp. Block timestamps come from a 544-point grid validated against freshly read blocks to within 1 second.
  • Provenance: two rounds of topic-filtered inbound scanning (413 sellers, then 143 second-level funders), then a five-hop walk following the largest source at each step.

What this does not establish

  • A deposit is an opportunity to sell, not proof of one. The convention that a non-Binance exchange deposit counts as a sale is applied consistently and is a convention, not an observation.
  • 49 wallets (1,017.8mn / $2,620,174) did not resolve within five hops. They are reported as unresolved with their immediate funder named, not assigned to a cluster.
  • The provenance scan starts at block 100,940,328 (28 May 2026). A wallet funded before that shows no inbound and is labelled as holding beforehand — 7 wallets, 56.2mn.
  • "Insider chain" means what §11 of the main report established — the named wallets in the 2025–26 distribution chain and the gas-funded clusters. It is not a claim about any person.
  • Exchange labels are OKLink entityTag reads. BscScan is behind a Cloudflare JavaScript challenge and could not be read from this environment.
The headline in one line. Of the 30,408.4mn released by the pools on 22 and 26 July, 40% by token count has since reached a non-Binance exchange — but almost none of it came from the wallets that claimed it. Three quarters of the value sold was float already cycling between exchanges, and the largest single identifiable pool-to-exchange flow is the 2,000.0mn from the June adminTransfer episode, worth $5,011,878 at the hour it was deposited.
