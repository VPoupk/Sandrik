The market-making operators of Request 1 sold on the exchanges, not into these pools

List 1 is four pools, one router and seven arbitrage contracts. Four entries are WETH/RSC pools (one on Uniswap, three on Aerodrome), and one is the OKX aggregator router. The other seven are not pools. They are the execution contracts of two arbitrage operations: they take RSC straight out of Gate and MEXC, sell it into the pools, and pass pool purchases to wallets that deposit them back at Gate (§2). The analysis below counts sales into the four pools. Routers and executors are followed through to whoever supplied the RSC.

Most of what was sold into the pools came from exchanges and from earlier DEX purchases, not from the project. Of 211.8M RSC sold, 44% had been bought on a DEX earlier, 40% had been withdrawn from a centralised exchange, and 10% was bought from one pool and sold into another in the same transaction. 6.0% traces to List 3. In both depreciation periods the largest sellers were exchange-to-DEX arbitrage accounts. One group sold 7,992,724 RSC into the pools in P1 and 14,357,909 in P2, drawn mainly from Coinbase and Exchange-scale B withdrawals (§9).

What does trace to List 3 is reward and donation RSC, not the market-making programme. 75% of it (9,476,723 RSC) was paid out by the reward-paying hot wallet, and 19% (2,438,791) by the Foundation Community Safe. List 3.3 accounts for 22 RSC, and List 3.2 for 379,622.

The single largest seller is a charity. 5,928,163 RSC (47% of the List-3-traceable total) was sold by Endaoment's two wallets, the Gnosis Safe 0xd90bea2b and ndao.eth. Endaoment is the 501(c)(3) donor-advised-fund platform ResearchHub partnered with in April 2025; it receives RSC donations from the hot wallet and the Safe and sells them through CoW Protocol to fund university labs (identified in the hot-wallet analysis). It sold 2,249,122 List-3-origin RSC in P1 and 1,916,512 in P2.

The rest is spread thinly. 282 other wallets sold at least 1,000 RSC of List-3-origin each (6,239 sold at least 1 RSC of it). For 247 of them, most of it came from the hot wallets, and for 27 from the Foundation Safe. Some sold every payment within minutes of receiving it. Two carry ResearchHub's "Contributor" label. Separately, 6,275,877 RSC of List-3-origin was added as liquidity to these pools rather than sold. About half of it came through wallets funded by a market maker ResearchHub itself labels as such; the team wallet 0x72f2c4dd and the Foundation Safe added the next largest amounts (§8).

The OKX router is a pass-through aggregator. All 91 of its RSC transfers are in the supplied file; Basescan's "Error in Internal Transaction" flag on 89 rows marks a failed sub-call, not a failed transfer. Executors A belong to one operation. Their RSC comes out of Gate (11,591,286 RSC straight from Gate's hot wallet into 0xa8a14ce2 alone) and out of the pools, and goes into the pools and to 0x96973f7b, which forwards every RSC it receives to Gate. 0x96973f7b is Gate supplier #5 in Request 1. The seven executors are treated here as those operators' own wallets, not as venues, so what they sell is traced back to where they got it.

3. The question, and what counts as a sale and a chain

The request: for every sale into the List 1 pools, particularly in P1 and P2, establish whether the RSC sold can be traced, through any intermediaries except the List 2 exchange wallets and across the Ethereum → Base bridge, back to a List 3 address, and show how the dates of receipt correspond to the dates of sale. List 2 and List 3 are identical, address for address, to Request 1's List 3 and List 4.

Definitions

A sale is the RSC leg of a Swap event in which RSC entered one of the four pools. Liquidity deposits (Mint events) are not sales and are reported separately (§8).

The seller is the wallet whose RSC balance fell in that transaction. Routers, aggregators and settlement contracts in between are looked through. A bot that buys in one pool and sells into another in the same transaction ends it holding no more RSC than it started with, so it is not a seller. That part is recorded as "routed pool to pool". Where a transaction had several sellers or several destinations, amounts are shared in proportion.

Origin is traced exactly as in Request 1. Each wallet's RSC carries a mix of origins, passed on in proportion to its balance, in time order, across both chains. The origins are a List 3 address (the last one the RSC passed through), an exchange withdrawal, or an earlier DEX purchase. Bridge mints are linked to the Ethereum deposit of the same amount; all but 3 of the 4,302 mints are.

Stated additions, as in Request 1

Exchanges beyond List 2 also break a chain: the two unlisted exchange wallets of the main document, and 0x0ba37f89, identified in Request 1 as Kraken's Base hot wallet. Swap venues break a chain as well: RSC bought from a pool is "DEX origin". §10 shows what changes when the Kraken wallet is treated as a wallet.

Every sale is valued at the RSC price of its day, as in the main document.

That is RSC the market-making operators and the Foundation Safe deposited at Kraken, reappearing when others withdrew it and sold it on a DEX. Under the List 2 rule this is not a chain.

Limits

A chain shows movement, not control. A seller with List-3-origin RSC received it from the project, directly or through others; that does not make the seller a project insider. Most List-3-linked sellers here are reward and donation recipients.

Batch settlement. CoW Protocol and aggregator transactions can fill several users' orders at once. Where a transaction had several sellers, the pool sale is shared among them in proportion to what each gave up.

Liquidity positions are opaque. RSC withdrawn from a liquidity position is treated as DEX origin, like RSC bought from the pool, because the position's contents change with every trade.
