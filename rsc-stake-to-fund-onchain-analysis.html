700M to a wallet that signs both large treasuries, 222.48M straight into Treasury 2, 70M to a vesting beneficiary, and 2.5M to a Treasury-1 signer. Two of the three 2.5M recipients are confirmed treasury signers. There was no public sale, no LP allocation and no airdrop at genesis — the float that exists today was distributed afterwards, out of these wallets. That is why nearly every depositor into the staking vault traces back here within two or three hops.

The 70M vesting arrangement, and what happened to it

VEST-BENEFICIARY-70M moved its full 70,000,000 RSC into a contract named Vesting (0x8bbB6c4B…ea02) on 2020-09-04. On 2022-01-04 that contract paid out in two directions and emptied: 16,041,666.67 RSC (22.9%) to the beneficiary and 53,958,333.33 RSC (77.1%) back to Treasury 2. That is a vesting termination with clawback of the unvested remainder. The detail that makes it interesting: a second vesting contract (0x4Df3043C…b662, still holding 2,755,068 RSC) names the same beneficiary and has a start date of 2022-01-05 — the day after. This reads as a re-papered grant, not a departure. The beneficiary kept 16.04M, was re-granted a much smaller tranche, still holds 8,090,687, and staked 5,000,000 of it in April 2026.

Two structural findings that carry real risk

(a) On Base, the vault address has no contract code. 0x467564…66d3 holds 3,323,117.53 RSC on Base (~$225k) at an address where eth_getCode returns empty, the account nonce is 0, and sampling historical blocks back to Base block 30,000,000 shows code has never existed there. Those tokens cannot move today. The address is CREATE2-derived — the Ethereum instance was produced by the canonical Safe factory via createProxyWithNonce, and recomputing that recipe reproduces the address exactly — so no private key exists for it and it cannot be an ordinary wallet. It is recoverable: the same factory and singleton are deployed on Base with identical bytecode, so replaying the deployment lands the Safe on top of the tokens, and because the salt commits to the owner set the transaction cannot be hijacked.

(b) On Ethereum, the vault Safe’s transaction nonce is 0. It has never executed a single Safe transaction in its life. No withdrawal path has ever been exercised or demonstrated for the 18,642,533.65 RSC it holds. (Its account nonce is 1, which is simply the EIP-161 default for a created contract — the two counters are different things.)

4. How the stake-to-fund flow actually works

Every single large deposit into the vault follows an identical three-hop choreography, executed within a 3–10 minute window. This is not a user calling stake() on a contract — it is an exchange-style deposit-and-sweep pipeline.

STEP 1 — depositor's own wallet (EOA) │ │ sends RSC to a freshly-deployed, single-use ERC-4337 smart account ▼ STEP 2 — DEPOSIT RELAY (ERC1967Proxy → "SingleOwnerMSCA" implementation) │ one relay per depositor; holds a zero balance before and after │ ├──── small "test" tranche, typically 0.1% ──────► HOT-WALLET 0x7F57d306…10cb │ └──── principal ─────────────────────────────────► STAKE-VAULT 0x467564…66d3 (never moves again)

A worked example, the 5M RSC deposit on 2026-04-20 (Ethereum, all timestamps UTC):

TimeMovementRSC
05:40:47VEST-BENEFICIARY-70M → relay 0x3b1fddbe…9e3f1,000.00
05:43:23relay → HOT-WALLET test sweep1,000.00
05:46:59VEST-BENEFICIARY-70M → relay5,000,000.00
05:50:11relay → STAKE-VAULT principal5,000,000.00

The relays are ERC-4337 modular smart accounts. Reading EIP-1967 implementation slots directly confirms that every relay on both chains points at the same implementation, SingleOwnerMSCA at 0xD206aC7fEf53d83ED4563E770b28Dba90D0D9eC8 — one uniform factory behind the whole deposit pipeline. They are ResearchHub-generated per-user deposit addresses — the on-chain footprint of the app’s “connect wallet and deposit” button. The routing split is informative: small amounts are swept to the operational hot wallet, large amounts to the cold vault. That is textbook custodial treasury management, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence that user balances here are an internal ledger rather than a contract position.

What this means for the “stake” abstraction

Because yield accrues on any RSC held in a ResearchHub account, there is no on-chain distinction between “staked” RSC and “deposited” RSC. The vault is simply where the large custodial balances are parked. A user’s stake is a database row; the vault is the backing. There is no on-chain enforcement of the yield formula, no lockup, no redemption guarantee, and no way for a depositor to exit without ResearchHub acting for them.

Net equals gross, and that is a structural fact rather than a behavioural one. The Ethereum Safe’s nonce is 0, meaning it has never executed a transaction — a Safe can only move tokens through execTransaction, so zero nonce mathematically forecloses any outflow. So what did the launch actually attract?

Of the $1.85M ever staked, $195,580 (2.04M RSC, 9.3%) came from outside the ResearchHub perimeter — and essentially all of it arrived on Base. The other 90.7% is the organisation, its treasuries, its grantees and its own multisig signers moving RSC from one pocket into another. On Ethereum the external share is 200,000 RSC — 1.07%, a single depositor who bought on Uniswap. A TVL chart for this product would be measuring internal transfers, not demand.

6b. What Treasury 3 actually does, and what that means for Tier B

Tier B rests on the claim that a depositor's RSC came from a ResearchHub treasury. That is true, but it does not by itself indicate any insider relationship — a treasury pays contributors as well as insiders. Treasury 3's full outbound history resolves which it is.

Of the 83 external recipients, 21 receive on a recurring monthly cadence — six or more transfers each with a median gap of 29–32 days. Between them they account for 9,550,551 RSC, or 66.2% of all external Treasury 3 flow. Several run for two years or more, and the per-payment amounts step up over time rather than varying with output.

That is the signature of recurring compensation — salaries, retainers or program stipends — not of per-review bounties, which would be irregular in both timing and size, and not of insider allocation, which would not need a monthly schedule. Bounty-style payments are handled by the hot wallet, which pays a far larger population (788 addresses) at a much smaller median size (850 RSC).

How this changes the reading

Tier B is compensation, not control. These wallets belong to people paid in RSC by the operating treasury over multi-year periods, who later staked part of what they earned. They are described here as treasury-compensated contributors rather than as insiders, and Tier B is deliberately kept separate from Tier A for that reason.

What does not change is the provenance conclusion. Tier B RSC still originated from a ResearchHub treasury rather than from the open market, so it does not represent external capital entering the token. The distinction matters for characterising the people involved, not for the flow-of-funds arithmetic: Tier A + B remains 19,925,849.65 RSC (90.7%), while the share attributable to control-linked wallets is Tier A alone, 17,574,849.65 RSC (80.0%).

One limit should be stated: RSC's USD price before August 2025 is outside the range the public CoinGecko API serves, so these historical payments cannot be sized in dollars here. The cadence and the RSC amounts are what the classification rests on.

The attribution does not hold, for two independent reasons.

  • The rally ran on net selling. Cumulative net taker flow across the whole window is −$112,236. If treasury RSC had been repurchased on-market at anything like the scale of the deposits, the tape would show sustained net buying. It shows the opposite.
  • The deposits and the price moves do not line up. The four days that produced the rally — 23 April, 30 April, 2 May and 8 May — carry $5.9M of the window's $11.4M volume between them and none of them has a staking deposit at all. Conversely the largest April deposit, 5,000,000 RSC on 20 April, landed on a $75,195 volume day. Acquiring that quantity on-market would have required roughly nine times the entire day's global turnover.

The rally is better explained by the pattern visible across the whole dataset: four isolated sessions of 15–60× median volume that move price 50%+ intraday and retrace within 48 hours. RSC returned to $0.0687 by 2 August, below where it started the window. What the deposit timing does confirm is the provenance finding — repeatedly, large stakes settle on days when the market could not have supplied them.

Insider traces: none to the exchanges, but one notable treasury→market path

Interpretation

The record establishes that roughly 7.92M RSC of treasury lineage has reached MEXC, Gate and Coinbase through single-purpose intermediate addresses, and that the routing is structured rather than incidental — three separate funnels, one per exchange, each retaining nothing.

It does not establish one-way disposal. The flow is partly bidirectional: distributor 0x620c0738 received 1,788,484 RSC back from MEXC 15, and MEXC-FUNNEL took 329,998 from Gate 1. That round-tripping is more consistent with market-making or exchange-side liquidity operations than with disposal. The supportable statement is that treasury-origin RSC is routed onto exchanges in volume, with the purpose unresolved on-chain. One structural observation holds under either reading: the Foundation Safe is the same entity that staked 1,483,316 RSC into the staking vault (§6), so a single treasury lineage supplies both the staked balance and exchange inventory.

The three divergences share one root: the hot wallet has a long operating history that predates the staking product by roughly two years, so lifetime turnover and post-launch turnover differ by a factor of about three. 9. Adjacent findings

BASE-DESK — the largest Base holder is not the deposit wallet

0x901Fc41a…480F holds 15,845,066 RSC, the largest single balance on Base, but it has initiated only 6 transactions in its life. Its RSC history is 21 events: large round-lot flows in and out with three counterparties (0x1985ea6e…, 0x26b610a0…, 0x739120ad…) in amounts like 807,608.99 / 916,006.87 / 755,845.41 RSC. That is an OTC or market-making relationship, not user deposits. Two of those same counterparties later appear as depositors into the staking vault.

Address-poisoning against the ResearchHub cluster

BASE-DESK has received three zero-value transfers from 0x198566394a02488e1cf7529d4bcc39b1a7ef5c87 — a vanity address engineered to mimic its genuine counterparty 0x1985EA6E9c68E1C272d8209f3B478AC2Fdb25c87 (same first four and last three characters). This is a classic address-poisoning attack timed immediately after each real transfer, intended to get a truncated address copied from history. Worth flagging to the team as an operational risk.

A second never-moved Safe using the same deposit pattern

0x6D1dFd4B…3497 is a 2-of-4 Safe holding 1,006,756.37 RSC on Base and 563,440.49 RSC on Ethereum. Its entire Base balance arrived in a single minute on 2026-05-14: 100 RSC, then 1,006,656.37 RSC, from EOA 0xe028e57f…e582, which swept itself to zero in the process. Its Base nonce is 0 — like the staking vault, it has never executed a transaction. The test-then-principal shape matches the deposit pipeline in §4, but the principal landed in a different Safe with a different signer set, so this is a separate custody or settlement arrangement rather than part of the 21.97M staked. Flagged because a second untouched million-RSC Safe on the same rails is worth understanding before drawing conclusions about where deposits go.

Reading “from 0x0” correctly on Base

FOUNDATION-COMMUNITY shows 32,601,942 RSC arriving from the zero address across 20 transfers. On Base that is not a mint of new supply — the Base RSC contract is a standard OP-stack bridged token (l1Token() returns the Ethereum RSC contract, bridge() returns the canonical 0x4200…0010 L2StandardBridge), so zero-address issuance there is the bridge crediting tokens already locked in the L1 escrow. Genesis-mint reasoning in §6 applies only to the Ethereum contract, whose supply was fixed at 1B on 2020-08-01. An analysis that treats Base zero-address inflows as new issuance would double-count badly.

Spam-token noise on the vault

Of the 96 token-transfer events into the vault address on Base, roughly 90% are unsolicited scam-token airdrops (Planet, Spell, Crispy, Robots, Nyx, Unicorn, Aurora and others) sent to a visibly wealthy address. One of them is a counterfeit token also using the symbol “RSC”, which sent 1.00 unit on 2026-05-03. Any analysis that filters by symbol rather than contract address will pick up that fake. All figures in this memo filter by contract address.

  4. Resolve SAFE-BASE-1M (0x6D1dFd4B…3497, §8) — a second never-moved Safe holding 1.57M RSC across both chains, funded through the same test-then-principal choreography as the staking deposits.

  6. Reconcile against the endowment dashboard. The dashboard's reported total stake volume (~29.3M RSC) renders client-side and could not be read directly; on-chain vault deposits plus non-treasury hot-wallet inflow since launch total ≈24.6M RSC.   7. Watch for the first vault outflow. The moment the ETH Safe’s nonce moves off 0, the redemption mechanism becomes observable. That is the single highest-information event to monitor.
  8. Reconcile against ResearchHub’s reported staking figures once published — the delta between their stated TVL and the 21.97M RSC actually in the vault would be informative.

Provenance and caveats. Every balance, owner set, nonce, supply figure and transfer in this memo was read from Ethereum and Base chain state on 2026-08-02 via archive JSON-RPC and the Blockscout explorer APIs; no aggregator, dashboard or third-party analytics product was used for on-chain facts. USD figures apply CoinGecko’s daily close for researchcoin to the UTC date of each transfer — a daily close, not an execution price, so per-line values carry intraday error, largest around the volatile 24 April and 3 May prints. Wallet nicknames are descriptive labels applied in this document to observed behaviour; they are not ResearchHub designations except where the memo states the address is published in ResearchHub’s documentation. Attribution of wallets to the organisation rests on signer overlap, genesis-mint receipt and treasury funding paths, all of which are verifiable on-chain. No wallet is attributed to any named individual, because chain data does not support such an attribution. Addresses in the “not established” tier may well turn out to be exchanges or unrelated third parties. §11 records the questions that remain open.
