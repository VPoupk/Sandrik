The headline finding is that there is no staking smart contract. RSC “staking” is a custodial book-entry: users send RSC to ResearchHub-controlled addresses and the yield (“Funding Credits”) is credited off-chain in the application database. ResearchHub’s own documentation confirms the design — “no lockups, no gas fees”, “principal is revocable”, “RSC held in a ResearchHub account automatically earns Funding Credits”, with yield defined as Your Yield = RSC Emissions × (Your Held RSC / Total Held RSC on Platform). Nothing about that requires — or uses — a staking contract, and none exists on either chain.

The address you identified, 0x467564…66d3, is the destination of every large deposit, so your premise was right in substance. Mechanically it is a Gnosis Safe v1.4.1, 2-of-3, that has received 19 deposits totalling 21,965,651.18 RSC and has never sent anything out. It behaves as an omnibus cold-custody vault for the staking product, not as a staking contract.

The second finding is about who actually staked. 87.8% of the RSC in that vault came from wallets that trace back to ResearchHub itself — the genesis mint, the treasury multisigs, or wallets whose keys sign those same treasuries. The single largest depositor, at 9,999,000 RSC (45.5% of the vault), is the wallet that received 700,000,000 RSC in the 2020 token generation event and is itself a signer on Treasury 1 and Treasury 2. Measured as external capital attracted, the launch drew roughly 2.69M RSC (~$240k), not $1.8M.

Two structural findings that carry real risk

(a) On Base, the vault address has no contract code. 0x467564…66d3 holds 3,323,117.53 RSC on Base (~$225k) at an address where eth_getCode returns empty and the nonce is 0. No Safe was ever deployed there. Those tokens cannot move today. They are recoverable only if the owners deploy the identical Safe at that address via the same CREATE2 factory and salt — possible, but not done.

(b) On Ethereum, the vault Safe’s nonce is 0. It has never executed a single transaction in its life. No withdrawal path has ever been exercised or demonstrated for the 18,642,533.65 RSC it holds.

4. How the stake-to-fund flow actually works

Every single large deposit into the vault follows an identical three-hop choreography, executed within a 3–10 minute window. This is not a user calling stake() on a contract — it is an exchange-style deposit-and-sweep pipeline.

STEP 1 — depositor's own wallet (EOA) │ │ sends RSC to a freshly-deployed, single-use ERC-4337 smart account ▼ STEP 2 — DEPOSIT RELAY (ERC1967Proxy → "SingleOwnerMSCA" implementation) │ one relay per depositor; holds a zero balance before and after │ ├──── small "test" tranche, typically 0.1% ──────► HOT-WALLET 0x7F57d306…10cb │ └──── principal ─────────────────────────────────► STAKE-VAULT 0x467564…66d3 (never moves again)

A worked example, the 5M RSC deposit on 2026-04-20 (Ethereum, all timestamps UTC):

TimeMovementRSC
05:40:47VEST-BENEFICIARY-70M → relay 0x3b1fddbe…9e3f1,000.00
05:43:23relay → HOT-WALLET test sweep1,000.00
05:46:59VEST-BENEFICIARY-70M → relay5,000,000.00
05:50:11relay → STAKE-VAULT principal5,000,000.00

The relays are ERC-4337 modular smart accounts. Reading EIP-1967 implementation slots directly confirms that every relay on both chains points at the same implementation, SingleOwnerMSCA at 0xD206aC7fEf53d83ED4563E770b28Dba90D0D9eC8 — one uniform factory behind the whole deposit pipeline. They are ResearchHub-generated per-user deposit addresses — the on-chain footprint of the app’s “connect wallet and deposit” button. The routing split is informative: small amounts are swept to the operational hot wallet, large amounts to the cold vault. That is textbook custodial treasury management, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence that user balances here are an internal ledger rather than a contract position.

What this means for the “stake” abstraction

Because yield accrues on any RSC held in a ResearchHub account, there is no on-chain distinction between “staked” RSC and “deposited” RSC. The vault is simply where the large custodial balances are parked. A user’s stake is a database row; the vault is the backing. There is no on-chain enforcement of the yield formula, no lockup, no redemption guarantee, and no way for a depositor to exit without ResearchHub acting for them.

Net equals gross, and that is a structural fact rather than a behavioural one. The Ethereum Safe’s nonce is 0, meaning it has never executed a transaction — a Safe can only move tokens through execTransaction, so zero nonce mathematically forecloses any outflow. On Base the address has no code and therefore no ability to transact at all. Retention is 100% because exit is currently impossible, not because depositors chose to stay. Read the “no withdrawals” number as a lock, not as loyalty.

So what did the launch actually attract?

Of the $1.80M staked after 10 April, roughly $240k (2.69M RSC) came from wallets with no traceable ResearchHub origin — and even that is generous, since three of those (BASE-DESK-C, BASE-DISTRIBUTOR-A/B) behave like exchange or market-maker infrastructure rather than retail participants. The other 87.8% is the organisation, its treasuries, its grantees and its own multisig signers moving RSC from one pocket into another. A TVL chart for this product would be measuring internal transfers, not demand.

The float discrepancy worth flagging

CoinGecko reports circulating supply of 238,228,884 RSC and a market cap of ~$67.0M. My residual float after removing every identified treasury, genesis, vesting and vault balance is ≈133.5M RSC — about 56% of the reported figure. 8. Adjacent findings

BASE-DESK — the largest Base holder is not the deposit wallet

0x901Fc41a…480F holds 15,845,066 RSC, the largest single balance on Base, but it has initiated only 6 transactions in its life. Its RSC history is 21 events: large round-lot flows in and out with three counterparties (0x1985ea6e…, 0x26b610a0…, 0x739120ad…) in amounts like 807,608.99 / 916,006.87 / 755,845.41 RSC. That is an OTC or market-making relationship, not user deposits. Two of those same counterparties later appear as depositors into the staking vault.

Address-poisoning against the ResearchHub cluster

BASE-DESK has received three zero-value transfers from 0x198566394a02488e1cf7529d4bcc39b1a7ef5c87 — a vanity address engineered to mimic its genuine counterparty 0x1985EA6E9c68E1C272d8209f3B478AC2Fdb25c87 (same first four and last three characters). This is a classic address-poisoning attack timed immediately after each real transfer, intended to get a truncated address copied from history. Worth flagging to the team as an operational risk.

A second never-moved Safe using the same deposit pattern

0x6D1dFd4B…3497 is a 2-of-4 Safe holding 1,006,756.37 RSC on Base and 563,440.49 RSC on Ethereum. Its entire Base balance arrived in a single minute on 2026-05-14: 100 RSC, then 1,006,656.37 RSC, from EOA 0xe028e57f…e582, which swept itself to zero in the process. Its Base nonce is 0 — like the staking vault, it has never executed a transaction. The test-then-principal shape matches the deposit pipeline in §4, but the principal landed in a different Safe with a different signer set, so this is a separate custody or settlement arrangement rather than part of the 21.97M staked. Flagged because a second untouched million-RSC Safe on the same rails is worth understanding before drawing conclusions about where deposits go.

Reading “from 0x0” correctly on Base

FOUNDATION-COMMUNITY shows 32,601,942 RSC arriving from the zero address across 20 transfers. On Base that is not a mint of new supply — the Base RSC contract is a standard OP-stack bridged token (l1Token() returns the Ethereum RSC contract, bridge() returns the canonical 0x4200…0010 L2StandardBridge), so zero-address issuance there is the bridge crediting tokens already locked in the L1 escrow. Genesis-mint reasoning in §6 applies only to the Ethereum contract, whose supply was fixed at 1B on 2020-08-01. An analysis that treats Base zero-address inflows as new issuance would double-count badly.

Spam-token noise on the vault

Of the 96 token-transfer events into the vault address on Base, roughly 90% are unsolicited scam-token airdrops (Planet, Spell, Crispy, Robots, Nyx, Unicorn, Aurora and others) sent to a visibly wealthy address. One of them is a counterfeit token also using the symbol “RSC”, which sent 1.00 unit on 2026-05-03. Any analysis that filters by symbol rather than contract address will pick up that fake. All figures in this memo filter by contract address.

  5. Watch for the first vault outflow. The moment the ETH Safe’s nonce moves off 0, the redemption mechanism becomes observable. That is the single highest-information event to monitor.

Provenance and caveats. Every balance, owner set, nonce, supply figure and transfer in this memo was read from Ethereum and Base chain state on 2026-08-02 via archive JSON-RPC and the Blockscout explorer APIs; no aggregator, dashboard or third-party analytics product was used for on-chain facts. USD figures apply CoinGecko’s daily close for researchcoin to the UTC date of each transfer — a daily close, not an execution price, so per-line values carry intraday error, largest around the volatile 24 April and 3 May prints. Wallet nicknames are my own labels describing observed behaviour; they are not ResearchHub designations except where the memo states the address is published in ResearchHub’s documentation. Attribution of wallets to the organisation rests on signer overlap, genesis-mint receipt and treasury funding paths, all of which are verifiable on-chain; no wallet is attributed to any named individual, because chain data does not support that and I have not inferred it. Addresses in the “not established” tier may well turn out to be exchanges or unrelated third parties. This is an initial pass — §10 lists what is still open.
