Headline — What Changed Between the Last Report (May 28, 2026) and Today

32.41bn AKE — 32.4% of total supply — left the eight project pool contracts on net in the nine weeks to Aug 1, 2026, and it left into the biggest price rally the token has ever had. AKE bottomed at a fresh all-time low of $0.00017407 on Jul 10, 2026 and printed a fresh all-time high of $0.00558723 on Jul 27, 2026 — a 32× move in 17 days. Three of the five pool contracts that had never been materially touched were emptied inside that window: Nodes Pool 3 went from 8.674bn to zero, Nodes Pool 2 from 6.710bn to 11mn, Nodes Pool 1 from 7.678bn to 642mn. Team Pool 2 moved for the first time in its existence (−2.000bn). And every single one of the five insider wallets this report previously flagged as "still holding" liquidated — four of them to zero.

Of that 34.48bn, 20.87bn went to 6,225 node claimants in flat ~3mn tranches, and 13.61bn went to eleven addresses. Ten of the eleven were created on 26 July, gas-funded within one hour of each other with identical amounts from a single exchange withdrawal address, and drew their allocations through a function that had been installed in the pool contracts the previous afternoon and did not exist before it.

Three corrections to earlier editions of this report, all settled on-chain this pass. (1) The pool outflows were not admin pushes. Of the 6,622 outbound transfers, exactly one was an admin push — the 4.073bn on 19 June. Every other transfer was pulled by its own recipient. How the Locked Pools Were Opened — Proxy Upgrades, Not Admin Keys

The pools were genuinely locked. They were opened by replacing their code. Every pool is an ERC1967 UUPS proxy: state lives in the proxy, logic lives in a swappable implementation contract. Twice in this window the controlling account upgraded a pool's implementation to a version that contained a withdrawal function the previous version did not have, moved tokens through it, and — the first time — upgraded straight back. This was verified by reading the ERC1967 implementation slot block by block, disassembling each implementation for the presence of the two function selectors, and reading the upgrade transactions themselves.

The two node-pool outliers are the clearest single finding in this report. Nodes Pool 1 paid 2,221 claimants a median of 2.941mn each, and paid 0x248e4158… 504.2mn. The two pools that received the extra code are precisely the two pools with a nine-figure outlier. Between them they took 1.540bn out of pools that were paying everyone else three million.

Cross-check against every wallet in the prior editions of this report

All ten July-26 wallets were checked against the union of every address appearing in the earlier full-history pool scans and in all three previously published pages — 1,447 addresses in total, including the 22 InvestorsPool recipients, the 70 KOL recipients, the 5,802 Nodes Pool 3 recipients and the whole insider set.

They are not the Investors 1–21 from the November-2025 to March-2026 tranches, they are not among the earlier node claimants, and they are not connected to the 13.8bn distributor chain. Every one is an address with no AKE history and no token history of any kind before 26 July 2026. Whoever these belong to, the entitlement was created on 25 July and taken the next day.

The Whale Insider's 833.2mn went straight into Binance Alpha on Jul 8 — the exact day Binance Wallet announced the Alpha Box airdrop covering AKE, and two days before AKE printed its all-time low of $0.00017407. The 3,196.5mn they collectively moved was worth $1.00M at the prices on the days they moved it. The same tokens at today's $0.00415901 would be $13.29M.

That cuts against the simplest reading. A cluster with advance knowledge of a 32× rally does not exit into the low. Two explanations survive the data: either this was ordinary capitulation by holders who had watched the price fall for four months, or — because Binance Alpha is a venue that holds inventory and sells it to buyers over time rather than filling instantly — the deposit date is not the realisation date, and what was actually realised depends on when Alpha buyers took that inventory. This report cannot distinguish those from chain data. It reports the deposit dates and the prices on those dates, and flags that the realised figure may be materially higher than $1.00M.

Methodology, Coverage and Limitations

How the chain data was obtained

  • Full Transfer-event scan of the AKE contract, blocks 100,940,328 → 110,819,786, in 49,999-block chunks, with automatic range-halving when a chunk exceeded the provider's 50,000-log response cap. 7,000+ distinct addresses observed.
  • Topic-filtered scan of all eight pool contracts (from-topic OR-filter) and of Binance Alpha in both directions, blocks 110,819,787 → 113,384,906.
  • Balance binary-search (eth_call balanceOf at successive block heights) to locate the exact block at which a wallet's balance changed, then reading that block's Transfer logs. This is how every hop of the provenance chain was settled.
  • Gas-funder tracing by binary-searching eth_getBalance to the first block a wallet held BNB, then reading that block's full transaction list to find the crediting transaction. Explorer "Funded By" fields were used only as a cross-check, never as the source.
  • Dating by eth_getBlockByNumber timestamps on the specific blocks cited, with linear interpolation between fetched anchors elsewhere.
  • All calls against a BSC archive node. Every checkpoint was written to disk after each chunk so the scans are resumable.

How prices were obtained

  • CoinGecko daily series for coin id akedo, 347 days, 2025-08-21 → 2026-08-01.
  • Every transfer is valued at the price for the UTC date of its own block. Nowhere is a single price applied across a set of transfers on different dates.
  • Cross-checked against Kraken AKEUSD daily VWAP over the 290 overlapping days: mean deviation +0.25%, median −0.04%. The two sources diverge by more than 10% only on days with intraday moves above 100%, where a daily figure is inherently ambiguous.
  • Transfers before 2025-08-21 are shown in AKE only. The token had no market.
  • Balance snapshots — as distinct from flows — are valued at $0.00415901 and labelled as such.

Limitations — what this report does not establish.
  • Identity. Nothing here names a person or a company. The strongest control evidence available is shared gas funding and transaction sequencing, which establishes common operation, not who operates.
  • The second layer under the node programme. The 20.9bn distributed on Jul 22 was traced one hop forward from a 40-address sample. The second layer was not traced to named venues; whether that 20.9bn reached the market and at what price is open.
  • Realisation versus deposit. Where tokens were deposited into Binance Alpha, this report values them at the deposit-date price. Alpha is an inventory venue that fills buyer demand over time, so the actual realised price may differ — potentially a lot, given the 32× move inside this window.
  • Coverage gap. The full address-level Transfer scan runs to block 110,819,786 (Jul 19). From there to the head, coverage is topic-filtered to the pool contracts and Binance Alpha. Large transfers between third parties in that final window would not be captured unless they touched a pool or Alpha.
  • Withdrawn figures. The previous edition's BubbleMaps exchange-cluster estimates and its "~$18.6M" for the Mega Forwarder are not reproduced. They were not reproducible against direct on-chain reads and are withdrawn rather than repeated.
