2 · Method, Sourcing and the Rules Used to Avoid Double Counting

What counts as evidence here

  • Chain data only, read by RPC at block-explorer level: eth_getLogs for Transfer events, balanceOf at specific blocks, eth_getCode for bytecode, eth_getStorageAt for ERC1967 implementation slots, full block transaction lists for gas funding, and simulated eth_call at historical blocks to date entitlements.
  • Wallet labels come from a block explorer's own page or they are not used. Every label in this report is an OKLink entityTag read directly from that address's page on 8 Aug 2026. BscScan could not be read this session and no BscScan label is relied on. Four routes were tried: plain HTTPS with a full browser header set (403), a TLS-impersonating client that reproduces Chrome's handshake (403, cf-mitigated: challenge — a Cloudflare JavaScript challenge), the Etherscan V2 API (BSC requires a paid plan), and a real headless Chromium, which is the only thing that can clear a JS challenge but has no network egress in this environment — every host, including example.com, fails with ERR_CONNECTION_RESET through the sandbox's egress proxy.
  • Project and exchange claims come from the primary source only — Akedo's own whitepaper and website, and Binance's own pages, announcement archive, Binance Square and Binance's public Alpha API (bapi/defi/v1/public/wallet-direct/buw/wallet/cex/alpha/all/token/list). All are binance.com. No aggregator, news site or analytics vendor is used for any factual claim.
  • Claims made off-chain are tested against the chain wherever the claim implies a chain footprint. The Binance Square airdrop announcement named three exact token amounts, so every AKE transfer in the relevant week was read and its exact values counted — a test that could have refuted the announcement and instead confirmed it (§10).
  • Prices: the CoinGecko daily series for akedo, applied per transfer date. Never a single price across dates — a flat price would misstate April by a factor of four on its own.
  • Where a controller cannot be established, that is said plainly. Six wallets in this report have no explorer label and no traceable funder, and they are described as unidentified rather than assigned a story.

Accounting rules

  • Sale = AKE received by a non-Binance exchange custody address, valued at that day's price.
  • Binance is reported separately and never as a sale. AKE has no Binance spot market — Binance's own page says so — so an Alpha deposit is not a sale into a Binance order book.
  • Exchange-internal legs are netted out. A transfer from one Gate.io wallet to another Gate.io wallet is not a sale; only deposits arriving from outside the venue set count.
  • Routers and aggregators are pass-through and are excluded from every total. A router receives and forwards inside a single transaction; counting it would double count the pool leg of the same swap.
  • DEX flow is measured net, as the balance the liquidity pools actually retain. Gross inflow counts the return leg of every arbitrage round-trip and is meaningless as a disposal figure.
  • Hold = AKE sitting in a wallet that is not a venue, however widely distributed.

That was a fair concern, and the previous edition did contain a directional error. This edition is built so the failure cannot recur, and each control is stated so it can be checked independently.

4 · Finding 2 — How the Locked Pools Were Opened: Proxy Upgrades, Not Admin Keys

The pools were genuinely locked. They were opened by replacing their code. Every pool is an ERC1967 UUPS proxy: state lives in the proxy, logic lives in a swappable implementation contract. Twice in 2026 the controlling account upgraded a pool's implementation to a version that contained a withdrawal function the previous version did not have, moved tokens through it, and — the first time — upgraded straight back. This was verified by reading the ERC1967 implementation slot block by block, disassembling each implementation for the presence of the two function selectors, and reading the upgrade transactions themselves.

6 · Sold or Held — Where the Tokens Actually End Up

Over the token's entire life, 75.04bn AKE has been deposited to non-Binance exchanges — $71.90M valued at each day's price. But only 1.90bn of that ($8.36M) arrived after the 26 July 2026 unlocks. The 32.41bn released from the pools in the last ten weeks has, so far, almost entirely not been sold. It is sitting still. Both halves of that sentence matter, and the second is the one that changes as soon as those wallets move.

The scan behind these numbers

Every AKE Transfer touching any of the 41 verified exchange custody or Binance addresses was read from the chain, from the token's deployment block (57,840,341) to chain head (114,777,002) — the token's entire history, with no gap. Each log was de-duplicated on (block, logIndex), so a transfer between two exchange wallets is recorded once, not twice. Why gross deposits, not net, is the sales figure

Exchange balances barely moved over the year — 2.42bn net across a decade of monthly flow — yet 75.04bn passed through. That is not a contradiction. When one holder deposits and sells, and a different buyer withdraws what they bought, the exchange's balance is unchanged but a real sale happened. Gross deposits from outside measure selling pressure; withdrawals measure buying and redistribution. Netting them would report a token that was never sold at all, which is plainly wrong.

The reverse caution applies too: a deposit is an opportunity to sell, not proof of one, and some of these deposits are custody moves rather than disposals. The convention used here — any non-Binance exchange deposit counts as a sale at that day's price — is the instruction this report was built to, and it is applied consistently.

The DEX channel is negligible

All 19 AKE liquidity pools across PancakeSwap and Uniswap — every fee tier, V2 and V3 — hold 70.8mn AKE between them, 0.07% of supply. Because each pool was created empty, that balance is by construction its lifetime inflow minus its lifetime outflow, so no scan is needed to establish it and none can contradict it.

One honest caveat: a pool's balance nets swap flow against liquidity provision and removal, so it is not a pure "sold into the pool" figure. But at 70.8mn the channel cannot be material on any decomposition — the deepest pool, PancakeSwap V3 AKE/WBNB at 0.01%, holds 63.1mn of it, and the other eighteen hold 7.7mn combined. Liquidity for a token with a $91M quoted market cap is close to nothing.

The finding that matters most for what happens next. 32.41bn AKE left the pools between 28 May and 8 August 2026. In the two weeks after the 26 July unlocks — the moment that made most of it movable — a total of 1,900.7mn ($8,358,718) reached a non-Binance exchange. That is 5.9% of what was released. What the chain shows is the airdrop being sold, not the airdrop being paid

Binance credits Alpha balances inside its own ledger, so the award of an Alpha Box airdrop has no on-chain footprint at all. What does appear on-chain is what happens next: the recipient sells, and Alpha settles that sale by routing tokens out of Binance custody into a DEX pool. Every one of these 1,305 transactions terminates at the PancakeSwap V3 AKE/WBNB pool. So this is 1,305 recipients disposing of an entire airdrop allocation, in one click, within hours of receiving it — 207.78mn of the 217.54mn on 8 July alone, the day it landed.

That also means 232.67mn is a floor, not the size of the airdrop. Any recipient who held their allocation inside Binance, or sold it against another Alpha user rather than into the pool, leaves no trace in this measurement. The true programme was at least this large and may have been considerably larger.

Why Binance receipts are still not counted as sales elsewhere in this report

Every non-Binance exchange deposit is treated as a sale at that day's price. Binance is excluded from that rule because AKE has no Binance spot market, so a deposit into Alpha custody is not a sale into a Binance order book. The same address both receives and pays out — over the token's life it has taken in 121.50bn and paid out 117.25bn against a total supply of 100bn, because the same float cycles through repeatedly. Treating gross deposits there as realised sales would overstate disposals by tens of billions of tokens. The airdrop analysis above is the exception that proves the rule: it counts only the 232.67mn that demonstrably left custody and terminated in a DEX pool.

13.4 · What they spent, and what the buyback would return

The node purchase is not visible on-chain from these wallets, and that is a finding rather than a gap in the scan. Every venue label used, and where it came from

97 addresses are classified as venues: 28 non-Binance exchange custody wallets, 13 Binance addresses, 19 DEX liquidity pools and 37 routers or aggregators. Exchange and Binance labels are OKLink entityTag reads from each address's own page on 8 Aug 2026. The 19 pools were not labelled by hand — they were enumerated directly from the PancakeSwap and Uniswap factory contracts by calling getPool() and getPair() for AKE against WBNB, USDT, USDC, BUSD, BTCB and ETH at every fee tier, which is why the set is provably complete for those factories.

Things this report deliberately does not claim

Two addresses previously described as "Binance Alpha Relayer" infrastructure They carry no explorer label. 0x653dd767…bacf560158 is a custom 552-byte proxy that resolves its implementation through an unlabelled registry. It is reported as an unlabelled contract.
Which allocation each pool corresponds to Six of the eight carry the OKLink tag AKEDO, confirming project control but not which allocation. The mapping to Investors / Advisors / Early Contributors rests on exact size match to the published tokenomics plus, for the Investors Pool, a textbook monthly unlock cadence. The Investors and Advisors implementations contain no name string in their bytecode; only the Nodes and Community pools self-identify.
The eight largest holders whose gas funder could not be found Listed as unresolved, holding 15,074.2mn between them, rather than assigned to a cluster.
Whether the 7.64bn deposited into Binance custody funded the airdrop Only that it arrived in the five days before, from wallets already flagged on other evidence. Custody is fungible and a balance cannot be earmarked, so no direct funding link is asserted (§10).
Whether any Akedo–Binance arrangement exists Nothing is claimed. An airdrop is a published Binance programme, not evidence of a private agreement.
Whether the vesting overrun breaches an agreement Only that withdrawals exceed the published schedule. A whitepaper is a statement of intent; the contracts demonstrably did not enforce it, because the code was replaced.
Whether an exchange deposit was actually sold A deposit is an opportunity to sell. The convention that a non-Binance deposit counts as a sale is applied consistently and flagged as a convention.
Known limitations.
  • BscScan was unreachable from this environment. It sits behind a Cloudflare JavaScript challenge; four independent routes were tried and are listed in §2. All labels this edition are OKLink's, and where BscScan and OKLink historically disagreed that disagreement could not be re-checked.
  • The airdrop figure of 217.54mn is a floor. Binance credits Alpha balances off-chain, so only allocations that were sold into a DEX pool leave a trace. Recipients who held, or who sold to another Alpha user, are invisible to this measurement.
  • The exact-value test covered 5–12 July 2026 only. Airdrop-tier amounts outside that window, if any, were not searched; a scan of 6–8 August found none.
  • The full-transfer ledger starts at block 88,000,000 (22 March 2026). Before that date the record is complete for every pool movement and every exchange or Binance movement — both scanned from the deployment block — but not for wallet-to-wallet transfers between third parties.
  • The label sweep covered the top 800 addresses by volume, 96.9% of all movement in the ledger window. The largest address left unlabelled moved under 79.6mn AKE in total, so an unlabelled exchange large enough to change any conclusion is not possible, but small unlabelled venues may exist in the tail.
  • DEX net absorption nets swaps against liquidity provision. At 70.8mn across all 19 pools the channel cannot be material, but it is not a pure "sold into the pool" figure.
  • Prices are CoinGecko daily averages. A transfer at an intraday extreme is valued at the day's mean, which matters most in April, when the price moved fourfold within the month.
  • Binance gross flow exceeds total supply (121.50bn in against 100bn issued) because the same tokens pass through Alpha repeatedly. It is a throughput figure, not a holding.

Reproducing this

Every figure is derived from public BNB Smart Chain state through a public archive RPC endpoint, using only eth_getLogs, eth_call, eth_getBalance, eth_getCode, eth_getStorageAt and eth_getBlockByNumber. The scan scripts, checkpoints and derived datasets are in pipeline/ in this repository. The three integrity controls in §2 — closed supply reconciliation, segment-tiling assertion and per-address chain reconciliation — are executable, not descriptive: they run as code and halt on failure, which is how the direction-reversal bug in the previous edition was caught.
