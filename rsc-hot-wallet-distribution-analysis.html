Three findings dominate. The payment population is small and highly skewed: the median payment is 850 RSC and 94% of all payments are under 10,000 RSC, yet 0.31% of transfers carry 36.1% of the value. Retention is low: 73.4% of recipients hold no RSC at all today, and only 13.9% of everything distributed still sits with the address it was paid to. And the largest non-bridge flows do not go to individuals at all — they run through a three-address pipeline that ends at a decentralised-exchange settlement contract.

2. The 1,818,217 RSC recipient

The single largest non-bridge destination in the hot wallet's history is 0xd90bea2b00b81a0c35dec7223fb1f20034674f8f, a Gnosis Safe. It received 1,818,216.85 RSC across 8 transfers. Prior payments to the same address do exist, but they are an order of magnitude smaller — the total is dominated by one transfer.

How the 13 May payment compares to everything else

At 1,179,666.98 RSC it is 1,388× the median distribution and sits at the 99.956th percentile — larger than 4,531 of the 4,533 payments the wallet has ever made.

It did not occur in isolation. On the same day, the Ethereum side of the hot wallet sent 1,200,000 RSC to the Base bridge — the second-largest transfer in its history. The bridged amount and the amount paid out on Base match to within 1.7%, and the two legs are hours apart. The 13 May payment was funded by a same-day cross-chain top-up rather than out of accumulated balance.

Twelve of the fourteen went to the Base bridge — the wallet moving its own funds between its two chains, not a payment to anyone.

4.1 How much was sold

What can and cannot be measured

"Sold" is not directly observable on-chain. What is observable is that 15.99M RSC no longer sits at the address it was paid to, and where the next hop went for the largest recipients.

Tracing the 70 largest recipients — 13.91M RSC, 75% of everything distributed — gives ≥3.48M RSC sent directly to decentralised-exchange venues (CoW Protocol settlement, Aerodrome and Uniswap pools) and 129,965 RSC deposited directly to a centralised exchange. The remaining 21.9M of onward movement goes to addresses that are neither, which for the top recipients means intermediate hops of the kind documented in the on-chain memo's exchange-funnel section. A precise total sold is therefore not determinable; the defensible statement is that at least 3.6M RSC reached a trading venue in one hop, and that 86.1% of everything distributed has left the recipient address.

The handover

The two large Base recipients do not overlap in time. OUTLIER-2 receives from the hot wallet between April 2025 and 14 March 2026, then stops. OUTLIER-1 receives its first payment on 24 April 2026 and continues through July. OUTLIER-2's own balance then flows into OUTLIER-1. The pattern is consistent with one operational function being rotated onto a new address shortly after the staking product launched, rather than with two unrelated counterparties.

A fourth pattern worth noting

0x4bdcf665…e68c received 867,017 RSC in a distinctly programmatic sequence: near-daily payments of 49,999.99, 74,999.99 and 99,999.98 RSC through late September and October 2025. Unlike the others, it still holds 866,265 RSC — essentially everything it was ever paid, making it the largest genuine holder among all hot-wallet recipients. Round-number amounts on a daily cadence, then no disposal, reads as a scheduled internal transfer or an escrow arrangement rather than a contributor payment.

6. Links to other wallets in the RSC structure

Each of the 896 recipient-chain pairs was checked against the set of addresses identified elsewhere in this work — the treasuries, the multisig signers, the staking depositors and their funders, the exchange funnels, the vesting contracts and the labelled exchange wallets.

Result: the overlap is real but small, with one substantial exception

12 of 896 recipient-chain pairs match a known address, and between them they received 43,632 RSC — under a quarter of one percent of everything distributed. Most are payments of a few hundred RSC, consistent with ordinary platform activity rather than with treasury movement.

The exception is at the pipeline. OUTLIER-2 received 480,851 RSC directly from the Foundation Community Safe across six transfers between June 2025 and June 2026 — including 300,000 RSC on 2026-06-03. That is a direct funding link between the project's own multisig and the largest non-bridge destination of hot-wallet funds, independent of the hot wallet itself.

The hot wallet is not the only address that pays RSC out to individuals. Treasury 3 (0xC4cfa2BdAE08416312fAa0B72758E1F3750f81e3) also makes direct payments, and applying the same census to it shows a different function entirely.

Treasury 3 runs a monthly payment schedule, not a bounty stream

Of Treasury 3's 83 external recipients, 21 are paid on a recurring monthly cadence — six or more transfers each, with a median gap of 29–32 days. Those 21 account for 9,550,551 RSC, 66.2% of all external Treasury 3 flow. Several run continuously for two years or more, and amounts step up or down over time rather than varying transfer-by-transfer.

That is the shape of salaries, retainers or program stipends. Bounty-style payment — many recipients, small amounts, irregular timing — is what the hot wallet does, at 788 recipients and a median of 850 RSC. The two accounts serve different functions and should not be pooled.

The two splits overlap — a monthly recipient can receive large payments — so they should not be added. What they establish jointly is that Treasury 3's external activity is dominated by a small, stable set of recurring payees rather than by a broad contributor population. 16 of its 83 external recipients also receive from the hot wallet, taking 5,911,292 RSC from Treasury 3 (41.0% of external flow), so the two payee populations overlap materially at the top even though their payment patterns differ.

What cannot be established here

RSC's USD price before August 2025 falls outside the range the public CoinGecko API serves, and the great majority of Treasury 3's payments predate that. These payments therefore cannot be sized in dollars, which means they cannot be compared against ResearchHub's published bounty values to confirm or exclude a bounty interpretation on amount alone. The classification above rests on cadence and recipient-set size, which are observable regardless of price.

Equally, a monthly schedule identifies a payment pattern, not a role. It is consistent with salaried staff, long-running editors, retained contractors or program grantees, and chain data does not distinguish between them.

8. Method and limits

  • Zero-value transfers are excluded throughout. 3,738 outbound transfers of exactly 0 RSC originate from address-poisoning activity by a third party rather than from ResearchHub, and their "recipients" are attacker-controlled look-alike addresses. Including them would inflate the recipient count by roughly 400 addresses.
  • The Base bridge and the burn address are excluded from the recipient population where stated. The bridge is the wallet moving its own funds between chains; the burn address is not a counterparty. Both are retained in the all-time distribution totals.
  • Retention is a point-in-time measure taken from current balances. It cannot distinguish a recipient who sold from one who moved funds to another wallet they control, and it does not capture RSC that was received, spent, and later re-acquired.
  • Onward-destination tracing covers the 70 largest recipients (13.91M RSC, 75% of distributed value), not the full population. Two of those traces hit the 300-event page limit and are lower bounds; the CoW Protocol figure for OUTLIER-1 rises from 895,069 to 1,074,703 when the sample is widened, so it should be read as a floor.
  • Address roles are inferred from behaviour and contract type. No recipient is attributed to a named individual or organisation beyond the labelled exchange wallets and the addresses ResearchHub publishes itself.

All figures derive from complete outbound RSC transfer histories for 0x7F57d306a9422ee8175aDc25898B1b2EBF1010cb on Ethereum (1,340 transfer events) and Base (7,569 events), cross-checked between two independent explorer API routes and reconciled against balanceOf at checkpoints. Recipient balances were read directly from each chain at the time of writing and will drift. Amounts are in RSC; no USD conversion is applied in this document.
