What drove RSC's 2025–2026 depreciation

A summary of what a year of on-chain and exchange-data research shows about the two depreciation periods since the centralised-exchange listings. It covers how the market was structured, who supplied the RSC that was sold, and why the stake-to-fund launch in April 2026 did little to change the trend. Each point is backed by one of five dedicated reports, cited by section.

We draw four conclusions. Each is labelled by how strongly the data supports it.

Conclusion 1 supported in structure · mechanism inferred

Liquidity was concentrated on the exchanges and thin in the on-chain pools, and that opened a wide, persistent arbitrage between them. The pools' average depth was 2.1M RSC-equivalent in P1 and 1.2M in P2, while exchanges held 25–30M RSC on Base. Project-origin selling into the pools, led by Endaoment's regular sales, was 1.9 to 2.8 times the pools' average depth in each period. Around it, over the two periods, wallets fed from exchanges sold 47.75M RSC into the pools and wallets that deliver to exchanges bought 20.17M RSC out of them. The measured facts support a mechanism in which regular pool sales set the price and arbitrage carries it onto the exchanges, however much liquidity the exchanges hold. One element is not established by our daily test: that arbitrageurs time their selling ahead of the regular sales and their buying after them (§3).

Conclusion 2 measured

Payments made by the project reached the pools in both periods, mostly through Endaoment and a handful of recurring payees. Endaoment sold 2.25M project-origin RSC into the pools in P1 and 1.92M in P2. Five other payees of the hot wallet, the Foundation Safe and Treasury 3 sold a further 1.17M across the two periods. Holders of large early allocations contributed mainly through exchanges and Ethereum rather than the Base pools. One allocation received on 1 January 2026 was sold on Ethereum pools and deposited at Gate within the month, inside P1 (§4).

Conclusion 3 measured

Stake-to-fund is detached from the market. At most 8.0% of the RSC staked since launch can be tied to a market purchase at any point. The product is a custodial ledger, not a staking contract, and the price rally after launch happened on days with no staking deposits. The 29.3M RSC reported as staked is about the size of all RSC held by exchanges on Base, yet it produced no sustained bid (§5).

Conclusion 4 not ruled out

A conflict of interest in the market makers' behaviour cannot be ruled out. A token loan that must be repaid in tokens rewards the borrower when the price falls. During its 18-month loan, the first market maker moved 12.56M RSC onto exchanges at an average of $0.52. It took 13.62M back at an average of $0.43, and then repaid 9,999,699 RSC worth $1,101,967 on the day. That pattern is consistent with selling high and repaying cheaply, but trades inside exchange accounts are not visible, so it can be neither confirmed nor excluded (§6).

3. Conclusion 1 — exchange liquidity, thin pools, and arbitrage between them

supported in structure · mechanism inferred

What was measured

  • The exchanges were supplied with far more RSC than their books ever showed. The median MEXC book held about $4,400 within 5% of mid before the launch and about $3,000 after it. A 2,250,000 RSC transfer on 28 July 2026 went into a book worth $1,752, and none of the market makers' MEXC deposits ever appeared as a matching offer (MEXC report, summary and §6.2). The deposits were absorbed without visible blocks. 98.4% of all MEXC trades by count, and 84.6% by value, were under $50 (§3.1). The report reads that distribution as a market-making programme recycling inventory in small prints.
  • The on-chain pools were thin. Measured from their token balances, the Uniswap 1% and Aerodrome ts 200 pools together held an average of 2.14M RSC-equivalent in P1 and 1.18M in P2 (September 2025 range 1.60M–2.64M). A third Aerodrome pool opened on 13 May 2026 and lifts the P2 average to 1.98M. In dollars the two pools fell from $1,506,965 to $134,963 across P1. Exchange wallets on Base held 25.51M RSC on the eve of the stake-to-fund launch and 29.74M at the end of P2, more than ten times the pools' depth.
  • Project-origin selling was large relative to that depth. In P1, 4,044,629 RSC of project origin was sold into the four pools, of which 3,498,270 had been paid out by the reward-paying hot wallets. That is 1.9 and 1.6 times the two-pool depth. In P2 the figures are 3,318,837 and 2,610,409, or 2.8 and 2.2 times (1.7 and 1.3 times against all three pools). The hot-wallet-origin sales across both periods, 6.11M, are about three times the pools' September 2025 depth (Request 2 §6; §8 of this report for the method).
  • Most RSC sold into the pools came from exchanges or from earlier pool purchases. 36% of everything sold into the pools in P1, and 52% in P2, had been withdrawn from a centralised exchange. Most of the rest had been bought from a pool earlier or was routed pool to pool within one transaction (Request 2 §5). The largest sellers in both periods withdrew RSC from Coinbase and an unlisted exchange and sold it into the pools. The largest buyers took what they bought straight back to Gate, MEXC and Coinbase, and several of them are the Gate and MEXC suppliers identified in Request 1 (Request 2 §9).
  • The two legs of that arbitrage move together. Across the 304 days of P1 and P2, daily selling by exchange-fed wallets and daily buying by exchange-bound wallets have a rank correlation of 0.61. Over those days the first group sold 47.75M RSC into the pools and the second bought 20.17M. Endaoment sold 4.17M.

How we read it

A regular seller in a thin pool moves the pool price. An arbitrage that withdraws RSC from exchanges, sells it into the pools and returns bought RSC to the exchanges then carries that price onto the exchanges, where deep supplied liquidity absorbs the flow without a visible book. On this reading, depreciation tracks the ratio of regular pool sales to pool depth rather than the quantity of exchange liquidity. The ratios above are greater than 1 in both periods, and highest in P2.

What this does not establish

We tested whether arbitrageurs sell into the pools ahead of Endaoment's sales and buy back after them. Within each period, the data do not show it. On the 47 days in P1 when Endaoment sold, buying by exchange-bound wallets averaged 21,668 RSC, against 20,736 on other days. In P2 the daily rank correlation is −0.01, and selling by exchange-fed wallets is no higher on or before Endaoment days in either period. Pooled across both periods, a positive association appears (rank correlation 0.38), but it reflects Endaoment selling more often in P2, when all arbitrage activity was higher. The mechanism above is therefore supported by the structure of the flows, not by a timing link to any one seller.

4. Conclusion 2 — project payments reaching the pools

measured

Endaoment. The largest seller of project-origin RSC into the pools is Endaoment, the 501(c)(3) donor-advised-fund platform ResearchHub partnered with in April 2025. It receives RSC from the hot wallet and the Foundation Safe and sells it through CoW Protocol to fund research (Stake-to-fund report §8.3). It sold 5.93M project-origin RSC into the pools overall: 2,249,122 in P1 and 1,916,512 in P2 (Request 2 §6–7).

5. Conclusion 3 — stake-to-fund is detached from the market

measured

  • Almost nothing staked was bought on a market. Of the 21,515,651 RSC deposited into the staking vault between 10 April and 13 August 2026, 92.0% was never bought on a market by the wallet that staked it. 81.5% came directly from a ResearchHub-complex wallet, and a further 10.5% from contributors' own balances, which trace back to treasury compensation. The most that can be tied to a market at any point is 8.0% (1,729,204 RSC), and the exchange-withdrawal part of that is an upper bound (Stake-to-fund report §9.3). A separate, hand-traced method reaches 90.7% insider lineage (§1, §6).
  • It is an accounting layer, not a market position. There is no staking contract; the product is a custodial ledger. Of the 29.3M RSC reported as staked (confirmed by the team), 21.97M sits in the vault, 2.69M arrived through the hot wallet, and 4.64–5.25M has no matching on-chain transfer. Nothing has ever been withdrawn (§1, §2, §9.4).
  • The post-launch rally did not come from the deposits. The four days that produced the April–May rally carry $5.9M of the window's $11.4M exchange volume, and none of them had a staking deposit. The largest deposit, 5,000,000 RSC, settled on a $75,195 volume day (§6c).
  • The scale is comparable to the market's float, with no lasting effect on price. 29.3M RSC is about the size of all RSC held by exchange wallets on Base (29.74M on 16 August 2026, of which 15.8M sits in a single exchange cold wallet), and some 15 to 25 times the depth of the pools. The price nonetheless returned to its pre-launch level within four months.

6. Conclusion 4 — market-maker incentives

not ruled out

Three operators. The on-chain record separates three market-making arrangements, each fed by the Foundation Community Safe through its own relays. Operator 1 held a 10M RSC loan from November 2024 to May 2026. Operator 2 ran a "funnel" route to MEXC, Gate and Coinbase from September 2025, and Operator 3 a "deposit address" route from May 2026. Each put about 10M RSC onto exchanges (Historical report §4a). Their exchange-facing wallets are the largest Gate and MEXC suppliers. 25.97M RSC of their deposits traces back to the project through their relays, and 85–100% of what each of these wallets deposited came from the project (Request 1 §4). The RSC they put on exchanges was the project's own stock; there is no sign of RSC from other sources. In P2, market-maker-sourced RSC was 35.5% of all RSC deposited to Gate and MEXC, the largest single identifiable source (Request 1 §7).

Why incentives matter here. A token loan repaid in tokens rewards the borrower for any fall in price. RSC sold while the price is high can be bought back cheaply before repayment. The market structure in §3 makes that easier to do quietly: deposits leave no footprint in the book, and arbitrage spreads the price effect across venues.

What Operator 1's wallets show. Over the loan they moved 12,564,167 RSC onto exchanges ($6,563,137 at the price on each deposit day, an average of $0.522). They took 13,621,580 RSC back off ($5,839,083, an average of $0.429). The loan was repaid with 9,999,699 RSC at 16:49 UTC on 29 May 2026, after an afternoon of withdrawals from Kraken, Gate, Coinbase and MEXC into the settlement wallet. At that day's price the repayment was worth $1,101,967. The Gate-facing wallet made one more deposit afterwards, 360,000 RSC on 31 May, which it had received an hour before the repayment. There are none after that.

Why this stays at "not ruled out"

Deposits and withdrawals are not trades. What happened to the RSC inside the exchange accounts, and at what prices, is not visible on-chain, so the figures above bound what the operator could have gained rather than measure it. Nothing in the data demonstrates self-interested trading, and nothing in it excludes it. The same applies to Operators 2 and 3, whose arrangements are still running.

7. What each report contributes

Historical distribution and flows

  • Identified the 16 addresses treated as ResearchHub, "the complex", and separated 11 more that belong to the market makers. Both groups were cross-checked with the team (§11, §4a).
  • Identified the recipient cohorts, including large allocations, recurring compensation, one-off grants and reward recipients, and what each did with its RSC. These were the starting list of likely primary sellers (§5).
  • Built the venue and volume timeline used to set the depreciation periods and to decide which exchange deposit histories to reconstruct first (§8).
  • Showed that ResearchHub's own trading nets to about zero: $1,816,617 sold against $1,849,666 bought over six years, a net of −$33,050 (§6.2, §6.5). That moved the question to external market factors.

MEXC order book and trade tape

  • The market makers' deposits to MEXC never appear as visible offers. After a 2.25M RSC deposit the largest offer shown was 51,811 RSC, and the exchange's balance grew by almost exactly the deposit (§6.2).
  • 98.4% of trades by count and 84.6% of value were under $50 (§3.1). Before April 2026 the trade rate was generated rather than observed (§3.2).

Request 1 — exchange suppliers

  • Of the 74 addresses that supplied more than 100,000 RSC to Gate or MEXC, 10 have a provable chain back to project sources, and 9 are pipes: 85–100% of what each deposited came from the project, usually within two days of receipt (§1, §4).
  • Those 9 are mostly market-maker wallets; the rest are fed by allocation holders. Project-origin RSC was 29.85M (32.3%) of what the 74 deposited, and 35.5% of all Gate and MEXC deposits in P2 (§6, §7).

Request 2 — DEX pool sales

  • List 1 resolved into four pools, a router and seven arbitrage executors. 211.8M RSC was sold into the pools in total. That is mostly secondary turnover, so it should not be compared directly with primary sales (§2, §5).
  • 12.67M RSC of project origin was sold into the pools: 4.04M in P1 and 3.32M in P2, 58% of it inside the two periods. The reward-paying hot wallet is the source of 75% of it, and Endaoment is the largest seller (§6).
  • Exchange-withdrawn RSC was 36% of all pool sales in P1 and 52% in P2 (§5), which is the cross-venue loop behind Conclusion 1.

Stake-to-fund on-chain analysis

  • At most 8.0% of the RSC staked since launch can be tied to a market purchase (§9.3). The product is a custodial ledger (§2), and the rally did not coincide with deposits (§6c).

8. Limits and method

Limits

Exchange accounts are opaque. On-chain data shows RSC arriving at and leaving exchanges, not what was traded inside them. Every statement about exchange-side behaviour, including Conclusion 4, is bounded by that.

A chain shows movement, not control. Tracing RSC from a project wallet to a seller establishes where the RSC came from, not who controls the seller. Most project-origin sellers in the pools are reward and donation recipients.

Depreciation has many inputs. This report identifies the flows that the data can measure and relates them to the price. It does not attribute the price path to them causally.

Method. Every RSC transfer on Ethereum and on Base was read from archive RPC logs, and the Base ledger was checked against the token's total supply to the wei. Origins are traced in proportion through each wallet's balance, in time order and across the bridge. Exchange wallets and swap venues break a chain, as the requests specify. Pool sales are the RSC legs of the pools' Swap events. Pool depth is the pool's RSC balance plus its WETH balance converted at the pool's own price, read at the end of each day. It matches DefiLlama's TVL series (Aerodrome Slipstream, Uniswap V3) to within a few percent on the dates compared: Aerodrome ts 200 $904,744 against $901,201 on 15 September 2025, and $128,006 against $126,423 on 31 March 2026. USD values use the daily RSC price of each transfer's day. The daily arbitrage test in §3 classes a wallet as exchange-fed if most of the RSC it ever received came from exchange wallets, and as exchange-bound if most of the RSC it ever sent went to them.
