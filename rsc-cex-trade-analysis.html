What the RSC trade tape can and cannot say about $1.8M of buying

A test of one specific claim, against 901,231 individual executions — 731,862 spot trades from four centralised exchanges and 169,369 on-chain swaps from three Base liquidity pools, covering 2026 to date. It tests whether the ~$1.8M staked could have been matched by buying in the market, and what the tape does and does not bound.

The staked RSC was never bought on the market. 90.71% of it traces to treasury lineage and was delivered from inventory — the path is treasury → depositor → vault, and no market transaction is required at any step. That reframes the question: the missing buy pressure is not an anomaly, it is what a treasury-delivered stake looks like. §11 sets this out in full.

Measured directionally, every flow runs the same way. The ResearchHub complex sent 4.96M RSC to exchanges and received 1.04M back — a net delivery of 3.92M. The Base pools absorbed 2.86M RSC net, of which 2.14M is one identified seller: Endaoment, the 501(c)(3) ResearchHub donates RSC to, liquidating those donations to fund university labs (§8.4). Exchange net taker flow was +310,313 RSC on 245M RSC of turnover, i.e. flat.

That does not settle the question, and this document does not claim it does. A buyer working passive limit orders is filled by aggressive sellers, so their accumulation prints as taker selling — and if they step the bid up, the price rises while net taker flow stays negative. That is exactly the pattern on the days that produced the April–May rally. Section 3 sets out the correct bound: takers sold $13.3M over the post-launch period, so the passive side bought $13.3M, and $1.78M is 13.4% of that — entirely feasible for a single participant.

The on-chain venues resolve part of it. RSC also trades on three Base pools, which are a third of total turnover, and an automated market maker has no passive side — every swap is directional. Through the rally, net on-chain buying was +$135,587, or 1,161,274 RSC removed from the pools, while centralised net taker flow read −$112,236. There was real net buying, and it is visible on-chain. The price move does not require an unexplained mechanism.

What it does not do is reach the figure under test. Net on-chain accumulation through the rally is 7.6% of $1.78M, and the combined CEX+DEX picture nets to roughly flat (+$23,350). Accumulation at the claimed scale remains possible, but it would have to have happened passively on the order books rather than on-chain. The price also ended the period 5.9% below where it started, having fully round-tripped a 2.4× rally, so whatever was accumulated left no durable price level.

The two datasets agree, and that is the strongest part of the finding

The on-chain memo established that 90.7% of the RSC staked came from wallets already holding it — genesis-mint recipients, the treasuries, and their own multisig signers — with only $195,580 arriving from outside that perimeter. The exchange tape, an entirely independent source, shows net market flow of roughly −$70k over the same window.

Both land in the same place: real external participation is in the low hundreds of thousands of dollars, an order of magnitude below $1.8M. The staked total was never bought — it was pre-existing inventory moved from one pocket to another, and the tape confirms the market never had to absorb it.

The exclusion turns out to have been the right call for a second reason

This section originally justified dropping MEXC as merely conservative — it is 40.7% of notional in the window where it does exist, so excluding it understates total turnover, while including it would only make $1.8M a smaller share of volume, not a more visible one. The full MEXC tape shows the exclusion was substantively correct, not just cautious. From the September 2025 listing to the end of March 2026 that venue printed 3,143 trades a day almost regardless of what was traded — 128 in every hour of the UTC day, with no session structure and a coefficient of variation of 0.01 across the whole of March. That rate times the mean print accounts for 97% of its pre-launch turnover. Adding MEXC to the denominator before April 2026 would have inflated it with volume that does not represent interest. The pattern breaks in April 2026, so MEXC notional inside this document's 25 June – 2 August window is far less affected. Details in “What the MEXC book shows, and what it cannot”, §3.2.

For completeness, across all four venues over 25 Jun – 2 Aug, net taker flow is +$195,829 — still nowhere near the figure under test.

Kraken's EUR book is excluded from USD aggregates because the guide is explicit that USD, USDT and EUR cannot be summed without a sourced FX rate. It is immaterial either way: at roughly EURUSD 1.08 it is ~1.3% of total notional. USDT is treated as ≈ USD for aggregation, which is stated rather than assumed silently — it is the one deliberate approximation in this analysis.

3. What "net taker flow" can and cannot prove

The side field marks the aggressor — the party who crossed the spread — not the maker. Net taker flow is therefore a measure of directional pressure, and it needs one honest caveat.

Why net taker flow does not answer the question

Every trade has a buyer and a seller, so an exchange tape can never show "net money in" the way a bank statement does. Critically, a buyer working passive limit orders is filled by aggressive sellers, so their accumulation is recorded as taker SELLING. If that buyer keeps stepping their bid up, the price rises while every print registers on the sell side.

That is not a hypothetical here. It is precisely the pattern on the days that produced the rally — 23 April rose 52.3% on net taker flow of −$117,968; 6 June rose 56.1% on −$3,717. Rising price on negative net taker flow is the signature of passive accumulation, not evidence against it. A negative net-taker figure therefore does not rule out a mandated buyer, and this document does not claim that it does.

The measure that does bound the question is the passive-side ceiling: whatever takers sold, the passive side bought. That sets the maximum any single accumulator could have absorbed.

6.1 The spike-and-fade pattern — the most striking thing in the data

RSC's big days share a signature that is worth flagging on its own merits. On each of the largest up-days, price rises 50–68% intraday on 15–60× median volume — while takers are net sellers. Then it gives most of it back within 24–48 hours.

The 6 June event is the largest single day of the year — $3.82M, 58× the median, of which $2.88M was Coinbase alone — and net taker flow was essentially zero (−$3,717) despite the price moving +56% with a high of $0.1827 (Kraken printed $0.2098). High volume, balanced aggression, a violent round trip: that is the profile of a thin book being worked, not of organic demand discovering a price. The same shape recurs on 23 April, where Coinbase alone shows −$163,922 of net taker selling into a +52% move.

Two readings are available and I cannot separate them from trade data alone. Either a large passive buyer was bidding the book up while aggressive sellers hit into them — which is real accumulation, but at a scale of ~$100–160k, not millions — or these are low-liquidity squeezes and wash-like activity. What can be said is that none of these events left a lasting price level, and none of them add up to sustained accumulation.

What the 7 July deposit does and does not show

On 7 July a single wallet staked 9,000,000 RSC — roughly $719,000, the largest deposit the product ever received and 40% of its entire lifetime intake. Total RSC turnover across every venue that day was $24,935.

Those tokens were plainly not purchased on the market that day — $719k would have been 29× the day's entire global volume — and the on-chain trace shows them coming from a genesis-mint wallet holding 60M RSC.

This bounds the timing, not the economics. Under an off-market-sale model the deposit would not coincide with any buying: a purchaser pays off-market, the treasury delivers tokens it already holds, and any repurchase is worked separately over time. The low-volume settlement day is therefore consistent with that model rather than evidence against it. What it does rule out is the simplest reading — that a staker bought RSC on the open market and then deposited it.

DEX flow is unambiguous in a way exchange flow is not

On an order book, net taker flow does not bound accumulation, because a passive buyer's fills print as taker sells (§3). On an automated market maker there is no passive side. Every swap is against the pool: if RSC leaves the pool someone bought it, if RSC enters the pool someone sold it. Net pool flow is therefore a direct measure of accumulation or distribution, with no interpretive ambiguity.

This makes the DEX data the sharpest instrument available for the question — and it is a third of the market, not a rounding error.

Method

Swaps were read from the pools' Swap logs. The RSC leg of each swap gives direction — RSC leaving the pool is a purchase, RSC entering it is a sale — and the WETH leg, valued at the daily ETH/USD rate, gives the notional. Because the pools are WETH-paired, DEX notionals are independent of the RSC price series used elsewhere in this document. Liquidity additions and removals do not emit Swap and are therefore excluded by construction. Block timestamps are interpolated across Base's fixed two-second cadence between measured endpoints, so day boundaries carry at most a few minutes of uncertainty.

8.4 The on-chain seller is identified, and it is a charity

The negative on-chain net flow from May onward is not anonymous. A single participant — Endaoment, the US 501(c)(3) donor-advised-fund platform ResearchHub publicly partnered with on 30 April 2025 — accounts for essentially all of it.

ResearchHub donates RSC to Endaoment from its distributing wallet and its Foundation Safe: 6,082,788.53 RSC across 34 transfers between April 2025 and July 2026, in every one of those 16 consecutive months. Endaoment's mandate is to convert those donations to cash and pay university research labs, and that is what the chain shows: 5,971,609.54 RSC sold through CoW Protocol in 2,811 fills across 133 days, median fill 1,654 RSC. Both of its addresses hold zero RSC today, and its inflows and outflows reconcile to exactly zero. The identification rests on bidirectional ENS resolution (ndao.eth) and on the Safe's own getOwners(); the flow figures are rebuilt from eth_getLogs across the full block range with no unrecovered chunks. The full derivation is in the distribution memo.

These sales are inside this dataset already

CoW Protocol routes to the best available venue. Sampling 25 of the 2,811 fills and reading their receipts, 24 contain a swap against one of the three pools measured in §8. The charity's liquidation is therefore already counted here as taker sell-side flow — it is not an additional, unmeasured source of supply.

9. Newsflow correlation

The one announcement that can be dated precisely from a primary source is the launch itself, via the post ID: 2026-04-10 16:51:29 UTC for the ResearchHub Foundation announcement, and 22:04:18 UTC for the "Funding Credits… around 24% in year one" follow-up. Both landed inside a single trading day.

The market reaction to that specific news was negligible: 10 April traded $232,521, roughly 3.5× the median day but well below the four spike days above, and closed at $0.0752 versus $0.0730 the day before (+3.0%). Volume in the surrounding days was higher before the announcement than after it — $1.91M on 8 April and $1.18M on 9 April, against $232k on the 10th and $100k on the 11th. Whatever moved RSC on 8–9 April, it was not this announcement, and the announcement itself produced no durable bid.

10. Caveats

  • USDT is treated as ≈ USD. The data guide is explicit that this is not strictly true. At the scale of the conclusion — an order-of-magnitude gap — a peg deviation of a fraction of a percent changes nothing, but the aggregate is a USD-equivalent, not a USD figure.
  • Base DEX pools are now included; Ethereum DEX is not. Section 8 covers the three WETH/RSC pools on Base, which carry a third of total turnover. The Uniswap V3 pool on Ethereum is not parsed here; it held 310,642 RSC at the time of writing and is materially smaller than the Base venues, but its omission means the DEX share stated is a floor.
  • Coverage completeness is asserted by the file, not proven. Per the data guide, observed min/max timestamps do not prove an unbroken interval. Every continuous venue shows trades on all 214 days, which is good evidence but not a guarantee against intra-day gaps.
  • Taker side is aggressor side. Restating the §3 caveat because it is the one genuine limit on the flow argument — the price and on-chain evidence carry the conclusion, not net taker flow alone. This has since been verified directly for MEXC, whose order book was obtained afterwards: the flag was tested against the book on 97,404 prints and holds. It remains an assumption for Coinbase, Gate.io and Kraken, whose books were not supplied.
  • Reported volume is not the same as interest. The full MEXC record shows that venue printing a fixed 3,143 trades a day with no session structure until April 2026, accounting for 97% of its pre-launch turnover. Nothing comparable was testable on the other three venues without their order books, so every share-of-volume and volume-normalised figure in this document should be read as measuring reported turnover, not demand.
  • Nothing here shows intent. The tape shows what traded. It cannot distinguish market-making from distribution, or identify who was on either side of any trade.

11. What the data supports

This section states only what the measurements establish, and marks explicitly where they stop.

11.1 The staked RSC was never bought on the market

Each of the 19 depositors was traced backwards through its own token history. 19,925,849.65 RSC — 90.71% of the vault — reaches a ResearchHub treasury, the 2020 genesis mint, or a wallet compensated by the treasury. The remaining 2,039,801.53 RSC (9.29%) came from wallets funded by exchange withdrawals or DEX purchases.

This is the pivotal fact, and it reframes the question. The path was treasury → depositor wallet → vault. No market transaction is required at any step. If a purchaser paid off-market, the cash went to the seller and the tokens came from existing inventory — an order book never sees it. Asking "where is the 22M of buy pressure" therefore presumes a market purchase that the chain shows did not happen. Its absence is not an anomaly to be explained; it is what a treasury-delivered stake looks like.

The limit of this test, stated precisely

Buying RSC on a centralised exchange does not require withdrawing it. Tokens purchased inside an exchange account can remain there indefinitely and leave no on-chain trace whatsoever. This measurement therefore excludes an on-chain repurchase; it cannot exclude one held at an exchange. It also covers only the labelled exchange wallets and the identified ResearchHub addresses on Base — a route through fresh intermediaries or through Ethereum would not appear.

11.4 There was real buying, and it is smaller than the claim by an order of magnitude

Through the 16 April – 12 May rally, 1,161,274 RSC was removed net from the Base pools (+$135,587) — unambiguous directional buying on a venue with no passive side. Every day that produced the rally shows net DEX buying. This is genuine demand and it explains why the price roughly doubled on a book this thin. It is about 5% of the staked quantity, and it fully reversed: RSC ended the period at $0.0687, below where the window opened.

Tracing provenance the other way — from the staking deposits back to where the RSC was acquired — gives a directly comparable figure for how much staking demand passed through a market at all. Across both deposit routes, 2,860,336 RSC of everything staked since launch was market-sourced, worth $262,146 at each deposit's own hour: 1,529,203 RSC straight into the vault (197,141 bought on the pools, 1,332,063 withdrawn from an exchange) and 1,331,133 RSC through the hot wallet (666,769 and 664,364 on the same split). The pool-bought 863,910 RSC is already counted in the DEX flow above; the 1,996,427 RSC of exchange withdrawals is an upper bound on purchasing, since a withdrawal cannot be distinguished on-chain from the return of coins deposited earlier.

The timing does not fit a buyer chasing the move. Market-sourced staking deposits carry a volume-weighted price of $0.09191 against a period time-weighted average of $0.09309, and the wallets that bought directly from a pool or an exchange did so at a VWAP of $0.08418 — 9.6% below the period average. In the two weeks around the 8 May peak, market-sourced deposits total under 3% of the period's market-bought total. The heaviest weeks are the launch week at $0.068 and the week of 5 June at $0.098. The on-chain memo sets this out with the per-deposit detail and a chart of the timing against price.

11.5 The volume increase is market-making, not accumulation

Turnover roughly tripled on exchanges and quadrupled on-chain after the launch, while net flow stayed near zero in both. Rising volume with balanced direction is the signature of two-sided quoting, and the treasury delivered 4.96M RSC of inventory to exchanges over the same period — the funding such an operation requires. The volume increase is consistent with a market maker being active. It is equally consistent with that maker hedging genuine off-market sales, so it does not discriminate between the two readings.

11.6 What cannot be determined from any public data

  • Whether purchasers paid real money off-market. The chain is identical whether an entity moved its own tokens between its own accounts or delivered inventory against payment received elsewhere. The payment leg is off-chain by construction.
  • Whether RSC was repurchased inside exchange accounts. See §11.3.
  • Who was on the passive side of exchange fills. Order books do not attribute counterparties; the ceiling ($13.3M post-launch) is a bound, not an identification.
Summary

Every measurable on-chain flow in the period runs in the same direction: from the treasury toward the market. 21.97M RSC of treasury-lineage tokens into the staking vault, 4.96M RSC of inventory to exchanges, 2.86M RSC net absorbed by the Base pools, and 86.1% of everything the hot wallet distributed no longer held by its recipient. Against that, on-chain buying during the rally was 1.16M RSC, and net exchange flow was flat on 245M RSC of turnover.

One component of that supply now has a name. 2.14M of the 2.86M RSC absorbed by the Base pools since launch is Endaoment, the charity ResearchHub donates to, liquidating those donations to fund university labs (§8.4). Net of it, post-launch on-chain net flow is −717,842 RSC rather than −2,856,059, and the year as a whole turns positive. That reclassifies part of the sell-side from unattributed supply to a disclosed programme; it does not change the quantity of RSC reaching the market.

The data does not show a buy-side programme of the size the staked total would imply, and it does not need one — the tokens came from inventory, not from the market. What the data cannot say is whether anyone paid for them.

12. Conclusion

The staking product coincided with a genuine and sustained increase in trading activity — turnover roughly tripled on exchanges and quadrupled on-chain, and has stayed elevated. That is real.

On the question of whether ~$1.8M of buying passed through the market: no such programme is visible, and the chain shows why none was required. 90.71% of the staked RSC came from treasury lineage and was delivered from inventory, so no purchase had to occur. Every measurable flow in the period runs from the treasury toward the market — 4.96M RSC of inventory delivered to exchanges against 1.04M received back, 2.86M RSC net absorbed by the Base pools, and exchange net taker flow of +310,313 RSC on 245M RSC of turnover.

There was real buying: 1,161,274 RSC removed net from the pools during the April–May rally, which is why the price roughly doubled. It amounts to about 5% of the staked quantity and it fully reversed, leaving RSC at $0.0687 — below where the window opened and 39% down on the year.

The reversal has an identified seller. Endaoment — the 501(c)(3) ResearchHub donates RSC to, so that it can be converted to cash for university labs — sold 3,362,102 RSC on-chain during 2026, against a full-year DEX net of −3,208,333 RSC (§8.4). Removing it leaves the year's on-chain net flow slightly positive. The charity was only 1.14% of sell-side during the rally itself, so it did not cause the rally; it is the dominant identified seller in the months that followed. This is an attribution of who was selling, not a claim about where the price would otherwise have gone.

Two things remain outside the reach of any public dataset, and no amount of further chain work will settle them: whether purchasers paid real money off-market, and whether RSC was repurchased inside exchange accounts without ever being withdrawn. Both are invisible by construction. The measurements bound the question; they do not close it.
