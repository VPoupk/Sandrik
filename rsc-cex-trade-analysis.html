Did the RSC staking launch pull $1.8M of buying through the market?

A test of one specific claim, against 731,862 individual executed spot trades from four centralised exchanges covering 1 January to 2 August 2026. If the stake-to-fund product genuinely attracted ~$1.8M of new capital, a matching bid should be visible on the tape. It is not.

Source: Tardis-derived trade tape, one row per executed trade — MEXC RSCUSDT, Gate.io RSC_USDT, Kraken RSC-USD, Kraken RSC-EUR, Coinbase RSC-USD
Companion to the on-chain memo; all staking figures referenced here come from that document

There is no trace of $1.8M of net buying. Since the 10 April launch, taker flow across the three continuously-covered venues is negative — aggressors sold $69,995 more than they bought, on $26,575,075 of turnover. The cumulative net-flow line never rises above +$5,190 at any point in the 115 days, and spends most of the period between −$100k and −$300k.

The claim under test needs ~$1.78M of one-directional accumulation, which would be 6.7% of all volume in the period arriving as persistent one-way pressure into a book whose median day is $65,751. That is not a subtle signal that could hide in the noise. It simply is not there, and the price went down over the window rather than up.

The two datasets agree, and that is the strongest part of the finding

The on-chain memo established that 90.7% of the RSC staked came from wallets already holding it — genesis-mint recipients, the treasuries, and their own multisig signers — with only $195,580 arriving from outside that perimeter. The exchange tape, an entirely independent source, shows net market flow of roughly −$70k over the same window.

Both land in the same place: real external participation is in the low hundreds of thousands of dollars, an order of magnitude below $1.8M. The staked total was never bought — it was pre-existing inventory moved from one pocket to another, and the tape confirms the market never had to absorb it.

MEXC contributes no data before 25 June, so it cannot appear in any before/after comparison without inventing a pre-launch baseline it does not have. Every headline figure in this document therefore uses the three continuously-covered venues only. That is a conservative choice in the right direction: MEXC is large (40.7% of notional in the window where it does exist), so excluding it understates total turnover — but including it would only make $1.8M a smaller share of volume, not a more visible one. For completeness, across all four venues over 25 Jun – 2 Aug, net taker flow is +$195,829 — still nowhere near the figure under test.

Kraken's EUR book is excluded from USD aggregates because the guide is explicit that USD, USDT and EUR cannot be summed without a sourced FX rate. It is immaterial either way: at roughly EURUSD 1.08 it is ~1.3% of total notional. USDT is treated as ≈ USD for aggregation, which is stated rather than assumed silently — it is the one deliberate approximation in this analysis.

3. What "net taker flow" can and cannot prove

The side field marks the aggressor — the party who crossed the spread — not the maker. Net taker flow is therefore a measure of directional pressure, and it needs one honest caveat.

The limitation, stated plainly

Every trade has a buyer and a seller, so an exchange tape can never show "net money in" the way a bank statement does. A patient accumulator working passive limit orders would be filled by aggressive sellers and would show up in this data as taker selling. So a negative net-taker figure does not, by itself, rule out someone quietly accumulating $1.8M.

What rules it out is the combination of three independent things: (1) taker flow shows no sustained bid; (2) the price fell over the period, whereas absorbing 6.7% of all turnover as one-way demand into a book this thin would lift it hard; and (3) the on-chain record shows the staked tokens never came from exchanges in the first place — they came from treasury-lineage wallets that already held them. Any one of these alone would be suggestive; together they are conclusive.

6.1 The spike-and-fade pattern — the most striking thing in the data

RSC's big days share a signature that is worth flagging on its own merits. On each of the largest up-days, price rises 50–68% intraday on 15–60× median volume — while takers are net sellers. Then it gives most of it back within 24–48 hours.

The 6 June event is the largest single day of the year — $3.82M, 58× the median, of which $2.88M was Coinbase alone — and net taker flow was essentially zero (−$3,717) despite the price moving +56% with a high of $0.1827 (Kraken printed $0.2098). High volume, balanced aggression, a violent round trip: that is the profile of a thin book being worked, not of organic demand discovering a price. The same shape recurs on 23 April, where Coinbase alone shows −$163,922 of net taker selling into a +52% move.

Two readings are available and I cannot separate them from trade data alone. Either a large passive buyer was bidding the book up while aggressive sellers hit into them — which is real accumulation, but at a scale of ~$100–160k, not millions — or these are low-liquidity squeezes and wash-like activity. What can be said is that none of these events left a lasting price level, and none of them add up to sustained accumulation.

The 7 July deposit settles it on its own

On 7 July a single wallet staked 9,000,000 RSC — roughly $719,000, the largest deposit the product ever received and 40% of its entire lifetime intake. Total RSC turnover across every venue that day was $24,935.

Buying $719k in a market trading $25k a day is not possible — it would be 29× the day's entire global volume, and would have moved the price by multiples. The price closed at $0.0798, down slightly. The tokens were plainly not bought; they were transferred from a wallet that already held them, which is exactly what the on-chain trace shows (a genesis-mint wallet holding 60M RSC). Whatever "TVL" that deposit created, it represented no new money entering the ecosystem.

8. Newsflow correlation

The one announcement that can be dated precisely from a primary source is the launch itself, via the post ID: 2026-04-10 16:51:29 UTC for the ResearchHub Foundation announcement, and 22:04:18 UTC for the "Funding Credits… around 24% in year one" follow-up. Both landed inside a single trading day.

The market reaction to that specific news was negligible: 10 April traded $232,521, roughly 3.5× the median day but well below the four spike days above, and closed at $0.0752 versus $0.0730 the day before (+3.0%). Volume in the surrounding days was higher before the announcement than after it — $1.91M on 8 April and $1.18M on 9 April, against $232k on the 10th and $100k on the 11th. Whatever moved RSC on 8–9 April, it was not this announcement, and the announcement itself produced no durable bid.

Scope limit on this section

I could not retrieve ResearchHub's posting history directly — x.com returns HTTP 402 to this environment — so this section rests on the two post IDs supplied or surfaced in search, decoded to exact timestamps, plus the docs. A systematic newsflow-to-volume correlation across all of 2026 is not something I can claim to have done. The spike days in §6.1 are identified from the tape alone and I have not matched them to announcements. If you can supply an export of the official channels' post history with timestamps, that overlay is straightforward to build and would be the natural next step.

9. Caveats

  • USDT is treated as ≈ USD. The data guide is explicit that this is not strictly true. At the scale of the conclusion — an order-of-magnitude gap — a peg deviation of a fraction of a percent changes nothing, but the aggregate is a USD-equivalent, not a USD figure.
  • Four venues are not the whole market. These files cover the venues where RSC's liquidity actually sits, but DEX volume (Uniswap V3 on Ethereum, Aerodrome on Base) is not in this dataset and is treated separately in the on-chain memo. A buyer could in principle have accumulated on-chain instead — though the on-chain trace is precisely what shows the staked tokens came from treasury wallets.
  • Coverage completeness is asserted by the file, not proven. Per the data guide, observed min/max timestamps do not prove an unbroken interval. Every continuous venue shows trades on all 214 days, which is good evidence but not a guarantee against intra-day gaps.
  • Taker side is aggressor side. Restating the §3 caveat because it is the one genuine limit on the flow argument — the price and on-chain evidence carry the conclusion, not net taker flow alone.
  • Nothing here shows intent. The tape shows what traded. It cannot distinguish market-making from distribution, or identify who was on either side of any trade.

10. Conclusion

The stake-to-fund product did coincide with a real and sustained increase in trading activity — turnover roughly tripled and has stayed elevated. That is genuine, and it is the strongest thing that can be said for it.

But on the specific question asked: there is no trace of $1.8M, or anything close to it, entering RSC through the market since 10 April. Net taker flow is −$69,995 across $26.6M of turnover; the cumulative line peaks at +$5,190; the best cherry-picked 60-day window reaches 13% of the required figure; and the price fell 5.9% over the window and 39% over the year. The single largest stake, $719k on 7 July, landed on a day the entire market traded $24,935.

Read alongside the on-chain memo, the picture is consistent and unambiguous. Roughly 90% of what was staked was pre-existing insider and treasury inventory that never touched an exchange, and the genuinely external share — about $195k on-chain, matched by market flow in the same low-hundred-thousands range — is what actually arrived. The gap between $1.8M of headline "TVL" and roughly $0.2M of real external participation is the finding.
