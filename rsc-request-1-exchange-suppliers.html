The 74 suppliers fall into two groups with almost nothing in between. Nine addresses are conduits from List 4 to the exchange: between 85% and 100% of what each deposited traces back to a List 4 address, and the RSC typically reached the exchange within a day or two of arriving. One more carries a trace of 1,373 RSC. Of the other 64, 61 have no chain at all, and three have a thin path that carries at most 4 RSC. Their RSC came from exchange withdrawals and DEX purchases (§8).

The project-sourced supply is overwhelmingly the market-making programme. Five of the nine conduits are the exchange-facing wallets of the three market-making operators identified in §4a of the main document. They include the largest supplier on each list: Operator 1's wallet 0xecd3b4b7 is #1 at Gate, and Operator 2's MEXC funnel 0x4Bae370A is #1 at MEXC. Together, List 4.3 addresses account for 25,970,072 RSC ($7,154,928).

Three allocation recipients sold through intermediaries. A team wallet (0x72f2c4dd) routed 2,284,230 RSC to Gate through 0xa6f1f230 between December 2024 and August 2025. That is before either depreciation period, but it is the second-largest dollar amount in this report. A wallet ResearchHub labels a market maker (0xa0ed916a) passed 300,000 RSC from the Safe to 0xddab9eaa within six hours on 23 December 2024, and it was on Gate two days later. And a 2,000,000 RSC allocation from Genesis 70M on 1 January 2026 (0x79aa5bc6) was partly sold on Ethereum through a TWAP contract. Another 378,580 RSC was bridged to Base and deposited at Gate on 8 January 2026, inside P1, seven days after it left the genesis wallet.

The project's own wallets appear directly once. The Foundation Community Safe sent 911,577 RSC to a single-use address on 15 May 2026. That address held it for 95 days and then passed it, whole, to Operator 3's Gate deposit address on 18 August 2026. From there it was on Gate within two days, just after P2 closed. Rewards paid by the hot wallets are practically absent from these suppliers: 1,375 RSC in total.

Look-through. List 4 counts a market-maker relay as a source even when what it passes on was inventory the operator had earlier withdrawn from an exchange. Tracing through List 4.2 and 4.3 to the project's own wallets (4.1) alone gives 25,298,418 RSC (27.4%, $7,827,148). That is 77.1% of what the ten linked suppliers deposited. Most of the rest, 16.1%, is RSC the operators had withdrawn from Gate and MEXC and sent back (§6).

2. The question, and what counts as a chain

The request: for every address on List 1 (45 Gate suppliers, 71,052,779 RSC) and List 2 (29 MEXC suppliers, 21,425,200 RSC), establish from the full on-chain history whether the RSC it sent to the exchange can be traced back to a List 4 source. A trace may run through any number of intermediaries, but not through the List 3 exchange wallets, and it continues across the Ethereum → Base bridge. The dates should show how receipt from the project corresponds to deposits at the exchange, with attention to P1 and P2.

Rules applied, as specified

Sources. The 63 unique List 4 addresses (16 in 4.1, 32 in 4.2, 17 in 4.3; two addresses appear in both 4.2 and 4.3). A List 4 address is the origin of whatever it sends. If RSC passes through two List 4 addresses, it is attributed to the later one, the point where it left the perimeter. §6 adds a look-through to 4.1.

Exclusions. A chain that passes through a List 3 exchange wallet is broken there: RSC coming out of an exchange is attributed to the exchange, not to whoever deposited it.

Bridge. A Base mint from 0x0 continues the chain on Ethereum when an equal amount (to 0.01 RSC) was sent to the L1 bridge 0x3154Cf16 within the 24 hours before the mint. The Ethereum side is then traced back the same way. All but 3 of the 4,302 Base mints matched: 3,432 to a send from the same address, 867 on amount and time only.

Three additions, stated so they can be challenged

1. Other exchanges and swap venues break a chain the same way List 3 does. That covers DEX pools, routers and aggregators on both chains; the two unlisted exchange wallets the main document already treats as exchanges; and 0x0ba37f89, identified here as Kraken's Base hot wallet. "Kraken 245" (List 3) has transacted with exactly one address, this one, 24 times in a weekly rhythm. All 98 addresses that ever deposited to it are sweep addresses that send to nothing else, the same signature as Gate (471 of 472) and MEXC (307 of 307). No other unlabelled address on Base with 20 or more depositors shows that pattern at a volume above 50,000 RSC. §9 shows the effect of treating it as a wallet instead.

2. Amounts are traced in proportion. When an intermediary holding a mix of RSC sends some on, each origin travels in proportion to its share of the balance at that moment. That is how the "RSC from List 4" figures are computed. Separately, a chain is said to exist when there is a time-ordered path of transfers of at least 1,000 RSC each from a List 4 address to the supplier. A path can exist and still carry almost nothing: §8 lists three such cases.

3. Every deposit is a sale at that day's price. USD values use the daily RSC price on the day of each deposit, as in the main document.

How the ten group

  • Operator 1 (main document §4a: the 2024 mandate). The Foundation Community Safe funded the market-maker allocation wallet 0xe4758f76 with 10,000,000 RSC on 24 November 2024. Four days later it paid 0xecd3b4b7 4,000,010 RSC directly, and on 2 December it moved 5,749,990 to the operator's second wallet 0xd8d6ffe3. That wallet made 87 smaller payments to 0xecd3b4b7 (5 December 2024 → 18 May 2025) and then passed its remaining 6,048,271 RSC to the settlement wallet 0xe92e6504, which made 17 more. The first Gate deposit came the day after the first receipt. The settlement wallet also fed 0x0d4d4296 at MEXC.
  • Operator 2 (the "funnel" route). The MEXC funnel 0x4Bae370A and Gate funnel 0x40a03e42 were fed by relays 0x620c0738 and 0x48cd88dA, mostly through distributor 0xAe823F1b. The relays were funded by the Foundation Community Safe. 0x620c0738 received 4,276,670 RSC in four transfers, 2 September 2025 → 20 January 2026. 0x48cd88dA received 4,999,990 on 28 July and 1,200,000 on 20 August 2026, and passed each on to the distributor within 20 minutes. 0x620c0738 also received 4,006,851 RSC withdrawn from MEXC, from 5 November 2025.
  • Operator 3 (the "deposit address" route, from May 2026). The Gate and MEXC deposit addresses 0x76785f12 and 0x6f12ae18 were fed by relays 0x74f191e4 (8,289,000 RSC from the Safe on 15 and 29 May 2026) and 0x0c86ac6c (1,710,900 from the Safe on 21 July 2026).
  • Allocations (List 4.2). 0xa6f1f230 ← team wallet 0x72f2c4dd; 0xddab9eaa ← 0xa0ed916a (ResearchHub: "on-chain market maker"; the Safe paid it 3,599,999 RSC in eight transfers, May → December 2024); 0x5c01a4e3 ← 0x79aa5bc6 (2,000,000 from Genesis 70M on 1 January 2026).
  • Trace. 0x6aef801d received 105,044 RSC from 0xdb3ded77, a busy wallet two hops downstream of a hot-wallet reward recipient. The share that follows through is 1,373 RSC.

Limits

Ownership is inferred, not known. A chain shows that RSC moved from a List 4 address to a supplier; it does not show who controls the supplier. For the five operator wallets the main document's §4a sets out the evidence that they are market-maker plumbing. For the others this report shows only the path.

Proportional tracing is one convention. First-in-first-out would give different splits inside mixed wallets. For the nine conduits the choice matters little, because 85–100% of what they held came from List 4.

Exchange accounts are opaque. RSC that a List 4 address deposited at an exchange, and that someone else later withdrew and deposited again, is attributed to the exchange, as the request's List 3 rule requires. Withdrawals are not ownership chains.

Scope. Deposits to Gate and MEXC on Base only, inside the date windows of the supplied files; Ethereum is traced only where RSC reached a supplier through the bridge. Sales on DEXs and at other exchanges by the same entities are outside this request. The 0x79aa5bc6 TWAP sales on Ethereum are one example.
