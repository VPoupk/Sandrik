We draw five findings. Each is labelled by how strongly the data supports it.

Finding 1 measured

RSC's spikes are market-wide, and arbitrage ties the exchanges and the pools together within minutes. In the eight 2026 spikes, every exchange and the pools crossed +15% on a 15-minute average within 70 minutes of each other, and in 5 of them within 11 minutes. No venue leads consistently, and the pools trailed the first exchange by at most 17 minutes. Five arbitrage operations made 31% of all wallet-level buying from the pools and 27% of the selling into them. One wallet bought 34.3M RSC from the pools and deposited all of it at Coinbase. A group of three wallets sold 36.2M RSC withdrawn from Coinbase into the pools and bought 12.8M back out of them. Their pool trades sit on the profitable side of the Coinbase price in 76–87% of the RSC they moved (§5).

Finding 2 measured

The RSC that arrives on exchanges during spikes is mostly arbitrage inventory bought in the pools, not market-maker supply. The one exception is 18 August 2026. Across seven of the eight 2026 spikes, the arbitrage wallets made 66% of on-chain deposits from the start of the run-up to 48 hours after the peak; the three market-making operators made 4%. Operator 2 made only 9% of its deposits inside the spike windows, which held between 13% (MEXC) and 58% (Coinbase) of each exchange's turnover. On 18 August, a new market maker's 7.0M RSC reached Coinbase between the two legs of a +31% spike, and 5M RSC in 1M lots left for MEXC, Kraken and a Gate-bound wallet within 2 h 34 min. Price fell 32% over the next 48 hours (§6).

Finding 3 measured cause inferred

Kraken was supplied by market makers and by RSC withdrawn from Coinbase. On-chain data does not support early holders as the source. The Foundation Community Safe deposited 299,983 RSC three days before Kraken's 17 November 2025 listing. Operator 1 deposited 1.1M on listing day and the day after. Operator 2's flows reached Kraken through a single forwarding wallet, and from June 2026 the largest supply was RSC withdrawn from Coinbase. Excluding a September deposit-and-withdraw loop, Kraken received 4.4M RSC net on-chain while its takers were net buyers of 0.7M RSC. The supply was absorbed through resting offers. Kraken traded a median 59 bp below Coinbase between spikes, and its book is thin enough that its top prints in a spike sit 12%–40% above its own 15-minute peak (§7).

Finding 4 new on-chain evidence

Five points change or extend the earlier reports. (a) The wallet 0x1695bf93… is a pool-to-Coinbase bot, not DEX infrastructure. (b) "Exchange-scale B" is very likely a Coinbase wallet. (c) A Coinbase-funded hub, 0x4b5c7108…, supplies the Coinbase-side arbitrage group, Kraken, MEXC and Gate, and once sent 404,163 RSC to Operator 2's distributor. (d) Two suppliers on the Gate and MEXC lists of Request 1 are 1M lots from the 18 August event. (e) The supplied Gate trade file reports the side of the historical-dump rows the wrong way round (§10).

Finding 5 not established

Whether any market maker sold into a spike cannot be shown from this data. Exchange accounts are not visible on-chain, and trade tapes carry no account identifiers. What can be shown is who held exchange inventory before each spike and who delivered more during it. Operator 3 had 6.3M RSC net delivered to Gate and Operator 2 had 7.3M net across the four venues when the 18 August spike began (§6.3).

What this establishes

The pools and Coinbase are kept in line by a small number of professional arbitrage operations that profit from the gap. When Coinbase rises, the bot buys in the pools and delivers to Coinbase. When the pools are richer, the Coinbase-side group sells exchange-withdrawn RSC into them. This is why a spike on Coinbase reaches the pools within minutes (§6), and why steady selling in the pools, mainly Endaoment's, reaches Coinbase. It does not tell us who the arbitrageurs are.

6.1 The spikes move every venue at once

In 2026 every venue crossed the +15% line within 70 minutes of the first, in 7 of eight episodes within 23 minutes and in 5 within 11 minutes. The slowest was 6 January, when Kraken moved first and MEXC followed 70 minutes later. The first venue varied: Coinbase 3×, Kraken 2×, MEXC 1×, the pools 1×, Gate 1×. The pools were never more than 17 minutes behind. A spike is a market-wide event that arbitrage spreads within minutes. It does not start on a single exchange and drift elsewhere. Run-up net taker flow is small next to turnover, as the CEX trade analysis found (§6.1 there). Large up-moves on these books happen with both buyers and sellers active.

Kraken's extreme prints are wicks. In the 2026 spikes its highest print exceeded its own 15-minute peak by 12% to 40% (median 18%). On 2 May a burst of trades reached $0.141 at 07:00 while Kraken's price around it was $0.103. Over the whole period Kraken's daily high was more than 5% above Coinbase's on 55 of 277 days (more than 10% on 25), while its daily low was more than 5% below Coinbase's on only 4. The $0.2098 Kraken print noted in the CEX trade analysis for 6 June is one of these wicks.

How far this goes

The chain shows the new market maker's RSC reaching four exchanges within hours, at the peak of a spike. It cannot show the RSC being sold, and the exchange hot wallets mix all users' funds. The link between the 7M deposit and the 1M lots rests on timing and round amounts, not on a traceable path. Both of the lots that appear in Request 1, 0x769e4e22… (Gate list, 3,000,000) and 0x53f39c47… (MEXC list, 1,000,050), are part of this event.

7.1 How Kraken was supplied

  • Before and at listing. The Foundation Community Safe (0xb280f00e…) deposited 299,983 RSC on 14 November 2025, after a 17 RSC test the night before. Kraken's RSC market opened on 17 November. That day and the next, Operator 1's settlement wallet (0xe92e6504…) deposited 1,100,200 RSC through 0x5845951d…, and took 402,106 back out on 18 November.
  • Operator 2, December 2025 – July 2026. 0xe2e69dce… received RSC only from Operator 2's wallets and sent it, the same day each time, only to Kraken: 1,618,363 RSC in 9 deposits. It is on the List 4.2 (allocations) list of Request 1; on-chain it behaves as Operator 2's Kraken leg. Operator 2 withdrew 648,469 RSC from Kraken in 8 transfers between April and July 2026.
  • The handover on 29 May 2026. Operator 1 withdrew 484,110 and 3,029 RSC from Kraken at 15:49–15:55, deposited 897,269 at 15:57 and withdrew 897,264 at 16:34, then returned its loan at 16:49. At 20:48 the same evening, Operator 3 deposited 400,000 RSC at Kraken.
  • From May, RSC withdrawn from Coinbase. Kraken deposits came mostly from wallets that had withdrawn RSC from Coinbase: 0x9b0c45d4…, 0x4b5c7108… and 0x00d46000… (1,501,074 on 25 August and 714,002 on 9 September). Coinbase's hot wallets also sent RSC straight to Kraken deposit addresses, mostly in lots of 5,000–20,000. Over the same months 0x92644069… withdrew 2.2M RSC from Kraken and deposited it at Coinbase. That is the direction Kraken's discount pays.

Excluding the loop, Kraken received 15.2M RSC on-chain since September 2025. By origin: 50% withdrawn from Coinbase, 26% market-maker wallets, 10% bought in the pools, 6% withdrawn from Gate or MEXC, 2% the Foundation Safe 0.9% hot-wallet payouts and 4% RSC withdrawn from Kraken earlier. Kraken paid out 10.8M, a net 4.4M.

7.2 Early holders

On-chain data does not support early holders as the source of Kraken's supply. Of everything deposited at Kraken since September 2025, 16,720 RSC (0.1%) had been held outside exchanges, pools and labelled wallets since before 2025. Hot-wallet payouts, the RSC that platform users earn and withdraw, were 0.9% of deposits outside the loop. No Kraken-labelled wallet ever touched RSC on Ethereum, where older holders are more likely to keep it, so an Ethereum route is not visible either. The caveat: if Kraken accepted Ethereum RSC through wallets that public label sets do not cover, those deposits would be missing here.

7.3 The September loop

From 15 to 26 September 2026, Kraken's hot wallet sent RSC to 0x15da7556… 42 times (6.1M in total), and 0x15da7556… sent it back to Kraken through one deposit address, 0xeb9ca8fd…, 42 times (6.0M). From 23 September the lots were about 147,000–148,700 RSC each way, the outbound and inbound legs usually 10–30 minutes apart, every one to six hours. In the same days 0x15da7556… received lots of about 81,000–107,000 RSC from the Coinbase-funded hub 0x4b5c7108… and forwarded most of them, within minutes to hours, to a different address each time. The loop is net zero for Kraken and its purpose is not visible on-chain. It is not selling: at its peak, 24 September, 1.93M RSC went round the loop while Kraken's whole market traded about 159,000 RSC. Any count of "RSC deposited at Kraken" for September must exclude it.

Reading Kraken

Kraken is a thin market that receives more RSC than it sends out. Market makers and RSC withdrawn from Coinbase arrive as inventory and are sold passively through resting offers. Over the whole period Kraken's takers were net buyers of 0.7M RSC while 4.4M RSC arrived net on-chain. That steady supply is consistent with Kraken trading a median 59 bp below Coinbase between spikes, though the tape cannot show who placed the resting offers. During spikes, aggressive buyers clear its thin offers and print the wicks of §6.1. The September 2026 discount followed August's net delivery of 2.5M RSC from Coinbase and came with net taker selling of 644k RSC. Calling this "oversold" fits the price; the on-chain supply behind it is market-maker and cross-exchange inventory, not early holders.

The MEXC report covers the venue in depth: a median book of about $4,400 within 5% of mid, a scheduled trade rate of 3,143 prints a day to March 2026, and market-maker deposits up to a hundred times the visible book. This report adds four points.

  • Listing-day seeding. The Foundation Community Safe deposited 84,145 RSC at MEXC on 20 September 2025. MEXC's first trade printed at 13:00 on 23 September, and Operator 2's MEXC funnel (0x4bae370a…) deposited 100,100 RSC at 13:06. Very little RSC held since before 2025 reached the exchanges in the period. Since September 2025, RSC held since before 2025 made up 4.0% of Coinbase's deposits (4.7M), 1.6% of Gate's, 0.5% of MEXC's and 0.1% of Kraken's. The largest single case:

One, 0x8b2864a6…, moved its allocation to 0xa8560f46… in December 2022. That wallet bridged it to Base and deposited 2,499,900 RSC at Coinbase through 0x5706bb8c… on 10 February 2026. The tape shows no matching burst of taker selling, and Coinbase's February net taker flow was positive, so the RSC was either sold passively over time, sold off-book, or not sold at all. Of the other two genesis allocations, one (0x95cccfee…) was sold into Ethereum pools in November–December 2023. The other (0x385ee67b…) was sent to 0xcfaf813c… in September 2020, which returned 1,562,500 RSC in 2020 and 2022 on a vesting-like schedule; it has not moved since.
  • Older hot-wallet payouts. 0x82fc9330… deposited 1,143,175 RSC at Coinbase in September–October 2025, most of it RSC that had left the hot wallet in 2022–2023. 0x34999701… deposited 565,629 RSC at Coinbase and Exchange-scale B in five lots between October 2025 and August 2026, all from a March 2024 hot-wallet payout.

10. What this changes in the earlier reports

10.1 The wallet 0x1695bf93… is an arbitrage bot, not DEX routing

Earlier reports grouped this address with the DEX routers. It is an ordinary externally owned account. It received 40.4M RSC straight from the Aerodrome and Uniswap pools in 16,374 transfers, starting 2025-07-31, and sent everything to Coinbase (30.2M) and Exchange-scale B (10.4M). It never sold into a pool, so Request 2's pool-sale attribution is unchanged. It is, however, the largest buyer from the pools since September 2025. Any statement in the earlier reports about RSC bought from the pools and delivered to exchanges is an undercount by this wallet's volume.

10.2 Exchange-scale B is very likely a Coinbase wallet

Two independent observations. First, the cold wallet 0x901fc41a… received 18.0M from Coinbase 35, 2.8M from Coinbase 22 and 2.8M from Exchange-scale B, and has only ever sent to Coinbase 35. Second, 27 of Exchange-scale B's 53 depositors also deposit at Coinbase, and they account for 97% of everything Exchange-scale B received. This is inference, not a label. Earlier reports that list Exchange-scale B as "exchange not identified" should read it as probably Coinbase.

10.3 A Coinbase-funded hub supplies the arbitrage group, three other exchanges and Operator 2

0x4b5c7108… has received RSC only from Coinbase (15.1M since July 2026). It sent 6.3M to the Coinbase-side arbitrage group in 676 transfers, 3.4M to the Kraken loop wallet, 1.2M to Kraken deposit addresses, 0.4M to MEXC deposit addresses and smaller amounts to Gate. On 22 July 2026 it sent 404,163 RSC to Operator 2's distributor 0xae823f1b…. Withdrawals from Coinbase to this one wallet fund the Coinbase-side arbitrage, cross-exchange inventory and, at least once, Operator 2's route. That is consistent with a single market-making operation behind several of the "arbitrage" wallets, but one shared funder is not proof of common ownership. An OTC desk serving several clients would look the same.

10.4 The supplied Gate trade file reports the side the wrong way round

The file maps the archive's side code 2 to "buy". Gate's own API says otherwise: for the 6,316 trades present in both the archive and the API (IDs 701,507–707,822), API "buy" is archive code 1, every time. A tick test agrees once the side is flipped: taker buys are on up-ticks 75.5% of the time and on down-ticks 18.1%. Any earlier use of the file's side column for the historical-dump rows (1 January – 1 June 2026) reverses Gate's taker flow. The CEX trade analysis used a different source and is not affected.

11. Method notes

  • Taker side. Coinbase's public trades report the maker's side and are flipped. Gate's archive code 1 is a taker buy. Kraken's b/s is the taker side. MEXC's flag was validated in the MEXC report. After correction, taker buys sit on up-ticks 72–82% of the time on Coinbase, Gate and Kraken USD, and 65% on MEXC.
  • Spike windows. A spike is a rise of 25% or more from the trailing 24-hour low of the hourly reference VWAP. Consecutive flagged hours within six hours are merged. The window for §6.2 runs from 24 hours before the low to 48 hours after the peak.
  • Deposit attribution. A deposit address is a wallet that sends at least 99% of its RSC to one exchange and is not mainly funded by pools or exchanges. A transfer into one counts as a deposit by the sender, at the time of the transfer. Origins are pro rata and time-ordered across Base and Ethereum, and the Ethereum-to-Base bridge carries the Ethereum-side mix (Request 1, §method).
  • Pool prices. Execution price per pool transaction is the WETH paid or received divided by the RSC, converted at that minute's Coinbase ETH-USD close. It is compared with the exchange's one-minute VWAP, or the nearest within five minutes.
  • Prices for USD values of deposits use the daily close, as in the earlier reports.
