Two files were supplied: a complete order-book snapshot series and the complete trade tape for the RSC/USDT pair on MEXC, from the first print on 23 September 2025 to 17 August 2026. Together they cover 327.9 days, 1,267,501 trades and 463,786 book photographs. This document is what they show when read against the on-chain record already assembled for this project.

The venue is far smaller than the flows that pass through it. The median RSC/USDT book holds 8,290 RSC on the offer and 13,925 on the bid within 5% of mid — about $4,400 of standing liquidity in total. On 28 July 2026, when ResearchHub sent 2,250,000 RSC to MEXC in one transfer, the entire visible book was worth $1,752. The deposit was roughly a hundred times the market it was sent into.

Most of the volume is manufactured. From the listing to the end of March 2026 the venue printed 3,143 trades a day, almost regardless of what was traded — a coefficient of variation of 0.01 across the whole of March, sixteen times steadier than the volume beneath it. Broken down by hour, March 2026 shows 128 trades in every single hour of the UTC day, with no session structure at all. Multiplying that rate by the mean print of $19.35 accounts for 97% of pre-launch turnover. The prints are real executions; the rate is a scheduler. Reported MEXC volume before April 2026 is not a measure of interest, and neither is anything derived from it — including the net aggressive flow of +0.58%, which a flat generator produces by construction.

The order book does answer the question it was fetched to answer — it just does not answer it in ResearchHub's favour or against it. Exactly one on-chain deposit can be matched to a resting offer with certainty, and that deposit came from Gate.io; every one of the twenty largest fills against it settles on-chain within 19 to 149 seconds, to the token. ResearchHub's own deposits leave no book footprint at all, and no matching outflow either. That is a real finding, and §6.2 sets out precisely what it does and does not license anyone to conclude.

What the absence of a footprint does not prove

ResearchHub's 5,719,278 RSC of MEXC deposits never appear as a large offer in the book. That rules out block distribution on this venue. It does not rule out selling: 5.72M RSC is 5.6% of the aggressive buying the tape carried over the same period, and an order sliced into $14 pieces would be indistinguishable from the surrounding churn. The honest statement is that the coins were not sold in a way that this data can see, and that no alternative venue or custodial ledger is visible from here.

2.1 Integrity

Both files are sound. The book arrives on a 60-second cadence — median interval exactly 60,000 ms, p10 54,100 ms, p90 67,101 ms. Twenty-six rows carry only one side of the book; all of them fall in the 51 minutes between the file's first row at 12:09 and the first two-sided book at 13:00:00.900 on 23 September 2025, which is the same minute the first trade printed. Discarding them leaves 463,760 usable snapshots across 327.94 days — 98.2% of a strict one-per-minute pull, with 2.8% of elapsed time falling inside gaps. The longest is 9.1 minutes; 47 gaps exceed five minutes and none exceeds ten. Across 20,000 sampled ladders there are no unparseable rows, no non-positive prices, no negative or zero sizes, and no crossed books. The tape has no duplicate trade ids and no zero, negative or missing prices or sizes.

The tape also reconciles against an independent source. Monthly turnover computed from the file matches MEXC's own public candle endpoint to the dollar or near it — April 2026 $1,927,775 against $1,927,775, June $3,390,625 against $3,390,624, July $2,377,776 against $2,377,767. The file is the venue's real tape, not a sample of it.

One field in the book must be discarded

Summing every published level gives a "total depth" that is meaningless here. The ask ladder reaches a median of 56,636% above mid — p10 34,902%, maximum 3,222,588% — because MEXC publishes resting orders at absurd prices alongside real ones. The very first snapshot in the file carries an offer at $2.00 while mid was $0.58. Every depth figure in this document is therefore measured within 1% or within 5% of mid, computed from the complete ladder rather than from a truncated top-of-book. An early pass of the deposit test using total published depth was discarded for this reason and re-run.

2.2 Establishing what the aggressor flag means

The tape carries an isBuySide boolean with no documentation. Rather than assume, it was tested against the book: for 97,404 trades with a snapshot inside the preceding three minutes, each print was compared to the prevailing bid and offer.

3.2 The trade count is generated, not observed

The size distribution above suggests an automated book. The timing of the prints settles it.

From the listing until the end of March 2026 the venue printed 3,143 trades a day, and almost nothing else. 81% of days between October and February fall within ±5% of that figure; 95% fall within ±15%. In March 2026 the daily count ranges from 3,018 to 3,169 across the whole month — a coefficient of variation of 0.01 — while the daily RSC volume over the same month has a coefficient of variation of 0.16, sixteen times larger. The number of trades is not responding to the amount being traded.

The intraday shape is more conclusive still. Every real market has sessions — Asian, European and American hours produce a pronounced daily rhythm in trade counts. Taking the median number of trades in each hour of the UTC day across a whole month:

In March 2026 this market printed 128 trades in every hour of every day — 127 in one of them — with no session structure whatsoever. On a sampled day, 1,439 of the 1,440 minutes contain at least one trade. A market does not do this. A scheduler does.

What this does to the volume figures

Multiplying the 3,143/day baseline by the mean pre-launch print of $19.35 gives $60,805 a day against actual turnover of $62,434 — 97%. Before the endowment launched, essentially the whole of MEXC's reported RSC turnover is accounted for by a constant-rate process. Only 3.7% of prints in that period sit above the baseline at all, and the days that do are the ones with identifiable real events: listing day (6,755 trades), and 10, 11 and 12 October 2025 — the three days of the Gate.io block described in §5.1.

After April 2026 the discipline breaks. A third of prints sit above baseline, the daily count becomes volatile, and a session profile appears. Whatever else the endowment did, real trading arrived on this venue around the time it launched.

This does not mean the tape is fiction. The trades are real executions between real accounts, and §5.1 shows individual fills settling on-chain within a minute. It means the rate is set by a process rather than by demand, that reported volume on this venue is close to meaningless as a measure of interest before April 2026, and that any statistic derived from volume — including the net aggressive flow in §4 — describes the generator far more than it describes the market.

Dollar turnover is flat — between $1.5M and $3.4M every month for eleven months. The rising RSC volume is the falling price, not growing interest. And net aggressive flow never leaves the band ±$55,000 in any month, in either direction, through a listing, an 89% decline, a product launch and an 83% intraday rally.

Read this figure narrowly

§3.2 shows that before April 2026 roughly 97% of this venue's turnover is produced at a constant rate of 3,143 trades a day. A generator that recycles inventory is flat by construction — it has to be, or it accumulates a position — so a net aggressive flow of +0.58% is close to what that process alone would produce. The flatness is therefore weak evidence about real supply and demand, not strong evidence that they were balanced. It does still bound the visible directional flow: no participant took a large one-way position through this tape without it showing, and none did.

5. Matching on-chain amounts to the order book

The test proposed was direct: take the on-chain transfers into MEXC's hot wallet and look for the same quantity appearing as a resting order. Every level of 50,000 RSC or more was extracted from all 463,760 ladders, giving 2,827 distinct price levels that ever held a block that size, grouped into 57 episodes. One of them matches an on-chain transfer exactly.

The quantities line up to the eighth decimal place. Gate.io sends 0.02 RSC as a test and then 700,000.89; the sum, 700,000.91 RSC, goes to MEXC; and 700,000.91 RSC is exactly the size of the offer that appears sixteen hours and forty-three minutes later — the largest single resting order in the pair's history. It is worked down through nine price steps over nine hours. At 17:58:59 the book shows 320,247.91 RSC remaining; six minutes later the hot wallet sends 320,246.91 RSC. The one-token difference is MEXC's flat withdrawal fee, which the test transfer confirms — 5.00 requested, 4.00 received. Two days later the whole remainder, 320,250.91 RSC, goes back to Gate.io.

379,753 RSC was sold, and 414,722 RSC of aggressive buying printed while the offer sat there — more than the offer shed, because other levels were trading too. The mid fell 19.0%.

The provenance is unambiguous and it is not ResearchHub. 0x55f6b366…bf06 was funded entirely by Gate.io, held the coins for three hours, forwarded all of them to MEXC and never transacted again. 0xde61bb21…ae06 received the remainder from MEXC and sent all of it straight back to Gate.io. Both are single-purpose wallets with zero balance today. This is an operator moving inventory between two exchanges, selling a little under 380,000 RSC on the thinner one and returning the rest — almost certainly the same operator on both ends, though nothing on-chain proves that.

The buyers can be identified too

The shortfall is consistently 0.010% to 0.025% — a taker fee — and one print differs by +0.084%, consistent with two fills settled in one withdrawal. Fourteen of the twenty went to 0xb80d90fc…df95, a wallet funded 12,847,461 RSC by Gate.io and 2,137,118 RSC by MEXC across 9,799 receipts, which forwards everything onward to DEX routers and holds nothing. This matters beyond the episode itself. It demonstrates that when a block genuinely trades on this venue, the on-chain record and the tape agree to the token and to the second. The method that finds nothing for ResearchHub's deposits in §6.2 is the same method that resolves this one completely.

5.2 508,394 RSC — a wall that priced the market without trading

The second-largest block behaves in the opposite way, and it appears 46 hours before the stake-to-fund launch.

Through 7 April 2026 RSC rallied from $0.0562 to $0.1026 — up 83% in fifteen hours, on a book carrying about 3,500 RSC on the offer. At 03:43 on 8 April an offer of 508,394 RSC appeared. Depth within 5% of mid went from 4,280 RSC to 512,177 RSC in five minutes, a 120-fold increase. Over the next 28 minutes the offer was repriced downward fifteen times, from $0.10001 to $0.08577, and the mid fell 14.4%. By 04:25 it was gone.

The 11 October block was sold. The 8 April block was cancelled — roughly 317,000 RSC left the book without trading, and the price fell 14% anyway. The display did the work, not the execution. RSC then recovered to $0.1130 later the same evening before falling to $0.0699 on launch day.

This block could not be attributed

No on-chain transfer into MEXC in the surrounding weeks matches 508,394 RSC or any combination near it, so the coins were already sitting on the exchange when the order was placed. Exchange-internal balances are not visible from the chain and MEXC does not publish order ownership. Who placed it is unknown, and no inference about ResearchHub is available from it. What the episode does establish is that the venue's price can be moved 14% by an order that never executes — which is a fact about every price in this document.

5.3 The systematic version

Applying the same fill-versus-pull test to all 57 episodes: 2,084,701 RSC left the large offer levels across the whole period, against 5,644,104 RSC of aggressive buying printed while they stood. In aggregate the big offers were traded through rather than pulled. The exceptions are individually identifiable — 8 April at 16%, 11 March at 10%, 30 March at 45%, 1 April at 46% — and only the first is large enough to matter.

One structural change deserves recording. Before the endowment launched there were 52 episodes of a 100,000+ RSC order resting in the book, peaking at 700,001. In the 130 days after it there are five, and the largest order anyone has shown since is 136,434 RSC. Whatever was placing size on this venue stopped in April 2026.

6.1 The traffic runs both ways

ResearchHub is not only a depositor. Distributor 0x620c0738…7eeb has taken 2,538,481 RSC out of MEXC across fifteen transfers, in tranches shaped like withdrawal limits — 399,999 twice, 349,998, 219,009, 104,999, 99,999 — each preceded by a token-sized test transfer minutes earlier. The wallet holds nothing. It is funded from the Foundation Community Safe (4,276,710 RSC) and from MEXC (2,538,481), and it forwards everything on to distributor 0xAe823F1b (2,808,598), the Gate funnel (1,294,445) and back into the MEXC funnel (914,311).

The clearest instance is late July 2026: 799,998 RSC withdrawn from MEXC on 22 July in two tranches, forwarded to 0xAe823F1b, and 2,250,000 RSC deposited back on 28 July. Whatever this is, it is inventory management across venues rather than a one-way disposal, and it means the gross deposit figure overstates any sale by a wide margin. The net is +3,180,797 RSC.

Outflow rose 32% above its ten-month baseline — a difference of roughly 257,000 RSC over the window, against a 2,250,000 RSC deposit — and the exchange's balance grew by almost exactly what was put into it. No buyer took delivery of anything resembling this quantity. That is consistent with the coins still sitting in the account, and it is also consistent with their having been sold to buyers who left the proceeds on the exchange; the chain cannot distinguish those. What it does exclude is a block sale to a counterparty who removed the coins, which is the pattern the one identifiable block in this dataset actually followed.

The three readings this leaves open

The coins were not sold. Consistent with the exchange's RSC balance growing by 4,904,564 over the period while ResearchHub deposited 5,719,278 net of withdrawals — the exchange gained almost exactly what ResearchHub put in, and every other depositor was net flat. This is the reading the data most naturally supports, but exchange hot-wallet balances aggregate all users and cannot separate them.

They were sold in slices. 5.72M RSC is 5.6% of the aggressive buying the tape carried; spread across eleven months in $14 pieces it would be invisible. Nothing here excludes this, and the flat net aggressive flow neither supports nor refutes it.

They were moved on. Sold for another asset, transferred to another MEXC account, or withdrawn to a chain not covered here. Only 2,538,481 RSC came back on-chain, so 3.18M is unaccounted for in either direction.

The endowment did not bring liquidity to this venue. Dollar turnover per day rose 29%, but the price fell 41% and the standing book shrank by a third in dollar terms while volatility rose. Counted in RSC the book looks deeper; counted in the currency anyone would actually sell for, it is thinner. Size stopped being shown almost entirely. RSC fell 19.4% on launch day itself, from $0.08673 on 9 April to $0.06992 on 10 April, and closed the data window at $0.05979 — 89.1% below the listing price.

The one measure that improved is price impact: the median absolute daily return per million RSC of net aggressive flow fell from 231% to 123%. Read alongside a book that is a third smaller in dollars, this says the venue is absorbing flow with less price movement than before while carrying less standing risk — the profile of an automated book that has widened its participation, not of new natural interest arriving.

9. Limits

  • One venue. MEXC carried $22.9M of the token's turnover over these eleven months. Coinbase, Gate.io, Kraken and the on-chain pools are not in these files, and nothing here transfers to them.
  • Order ownership is not published. Every attribution in §5 rests on matching quantities and timing between the chain and the book. The 11 October match is exact on both ends and eighteen hours apart; it is as strong as this method gets. The 8 April block has no on-chain counterpart and is not attributed.
  • One-minute photographs. Orders that appear and cancel inside a minute are invisible. The fill-versus-pull test in §5.3 therefore understates cancellation activity, and the "16% filled" figure for 8 April is an upper bound on how much of that block traded.
  • Exchange-internal balances are opaque. Coins already on MEXC before 23 September 2025, and transfers between MEXC accounts, are outside every dataset used here.
  • 2.8% of elapsed time falls inside gaps in the book file — 72 hours in January 2026, 43 in December 2025, 31 in March 2026. No episode discussed here falls in a gap.
  • §3.2 shows the trade rate is generated; it does not identify who generates it. Exchange-run market-making programmes are ordinary and disclosed at many venues. The finding is that reported volume before April 2026 cannot be read as interest — not that any particular party manufactured it, and not that the executions are fictitious.

10. Method

Reproduction

Book. The 3.81 GB snapshot file was parsed once into a compact per-snapshot record carrying best bid and offer, level counts, the 25 nearest levels each side, and depth bands computed from the complete ladder at 1% and 5% of mid. Total published depth was computed and then discarded once the ladder's reach was measured (§2.1).

Tape. A single pass over all 1,267,501 trades produced per-minute, per-hour and per-day aggregates of aggressive buy and sell volume in RSC and USD, plus every print of 20,000 RSC or more. No sampling is used anywhere in this document; an early sampled profile understated the largest print as 35,460 RSC against the true 82,860.79 and was replaced.

Chain. The RSC ledger already built for this project — 1,379,897 transfers on Ethereum and Base with no unrecovered block ranges — filtered to every transfer touching MEXC hot wallet 0x4e3ae00e…1b60 from 1 September 2025. 3,459 deposits and 3,047 withdrawals.

Prices. Every dollar figure in this document uses MEXC's own volume-weighted average price for the day of the event, computed from the tape itself, so on-chain and on-venue quantities are marked against the same source.

Block detection. Every level of ≥50,000 RSC across all ladders, grouped by price, then grouped into episodes separated by more than 30 minutes. Fill share is aggressive volume printed during the episode divided by the reduction in the level's size.

Order-book and trade data supplied as vendor extracts of the MEXC RSC/USDT pair, 23 September 2025 to 17 August 2026, and cross-checked against MEXC's public candle endpoint. On-chain data from archive JSON-RPC on Ethereum and Base. All times UTC. Quantities are stated as recorded; dollar figures are marked to the venue VWAP on the day of each event and are estimates.

Companion data: rsc-mexc-daily-series.json (per-day price, turnover, aggressive flow and near-touch depth), rsc-mexc-block-episodes.json (all 57 large-order episodes with fill shares), rsc-mexc-onchain-flows.json (every on-chain transfer touching the MEXC hot wallet).
