Q1 — When Did Investors Last Claim, Who Received From the Investors Pool, and Do Any of Them Cross With the Insider Set?

The real investor cohort stopped claiming on 26 February 2026. Nothing came out of the Investors Pool for the next five months. Then 8.073bn left it in two events, and not one token went to an address that had ever claimed before. Twenty-two wallets drew 5.054bn between August 2025 and February 2026 in investor-shaped tranches. The two 2026 events — 4.073bn on 19 June and 4.000bn on 26 July — went to five addresses with no prior AKE history of any kind.

Reading it plainly. The Investors Pool behaved like a vesting contract for two quarters — a TGE tranche, a cliff release in late November, a tail into February — paid to 22 wallets whose amounts look like real allocation sizes and which mostly sold down over the following months. Then the pattern stops completely. The 8.073bn that left afterwards went to five addresses that had never touched AKE, in two events that both required the contract's code to be changed first. Whatever the four July-26 recipients are, the chain gives no evidence they are the investors this pool was funded for, and it does show they were provisioned by the same operator as the other six July-26 wallets.

Q2 — What Happened to the AKE That Accumulated on Binance Wallets? Does Binance Only Hold 4.5bn?

Yes — and the more important finding is that Binance never held much more than that. Sampling balanceOf across the whole Binance cluster from launch to today shows Binance Alpha 2.0's AKE inventory has never once exceeded ~7.64bn, and every other Binance address in the cluster — the Alpha router, both relayers, and Binance hot wallets 9, 10, 12, 13, 20, 23 and 51 — has held exactly zero AKE at every point sampled, for the token's entire life. The 18.58bn April burst did not accumulate anywhere. It passed through.

So where did the throughput go?

Alpha is an inventory account for a buy/sell venue, not a vault. Tokens deposited there are bought by Binance users and withdrawn to the buyers' own addresses. Between 19 July and 1 August alone, 23.272bn AKE left Alpha to 117 recipients, of which only 1.857bn went back to Alpha's own infrastructure — so 21.415bn genuinely left the venue to external addresses in thirteen days, against an inventory that never held more than 7.64bn. The float turns over several times a month.

This corrects a framing in the main report. Earlier editions described "~14bn already sold to buyers" as though Alpha had accumulated and then released it. The balance curve shows something different and simpler: Alpha never accumulated. Deposits and withdrawals ran concurrently throughout, including during the April burst, when inventory actually fell on 15 April while deposits were still arriving. The correct statement is that Alpha is a conduit whose standing inventory has ranged between roughly 0.3bn and 7.6bn for the token's entire life, and sits at 4.517bn now.

Q3 — How Do We Know Team Pools 1 and 2 Are Actually Team Allocations?

The honest answer is that the bytecode does not say so — and the earlier editions of this report implied it did. Pulling the launch-time implementation for every pool and extracting its embedded strings shows that only two pools self-identify: the Nodes pools contain "CreaterPlayerNodesPool: checker …" and the Community Pool contains "CommunityPool: checker is zero address". The Investors Pool, both Team pools and the KOL Pool contain no name string at all, at launch or today. Their labels were an inference.

That inference does hold up — but on different evidence, set out below.

Method and Limitations

How each answer was produced

  • Q1 — Investors Pool recipients from the earlier full-history pool scan (blocks 57.84M–101.4M) joined to the verified pool reconciliation (100.94M–113.38M). Cross-referenced against 1,447 addresses from all prior scans and published pages.
  • Q2 — balanceOf sampled across eleven Binance-cluster addresses at 24 points from launch to head, then densely re-sampled every 150k blocks across the April burst and the July pump.
  • Q3 — launch-time implementation bytecode pulled for all eight pools and string-extracted; allocation sizes matched against published tokenomics; Team pool outflows from a dedicated full-history scan.
  • Q4 — merged transfer set across four scans, insider senders classified, destinations resolved to named venues, each transfer priced at its own date.
  • Q5/Q6 — every hop re-derived by binary-searching the wallet's balance to the block it changed, then reading that block's logs. The terminal step scanned separately as 30 individually dated Feeder→Alpha transfers.

What is not established

  • Identity. No person or company is named anywhere. Control is inferred from funding, sequencing and timing only.
  • Binance inventory sampling. The balance curve is sampled, not continuous — at 150k-block resolution across the two key windows and 2.5M elsewhere. A spike lasting less than a few hours between samples would not appear.
  • Advisors pool tail. The dedicated Team/Investors scan reached block 84.7M; the 54mn difference between the 1.805bn found and the 1.859bn balance delta to May 2026 falls after that point and was not itemised.
  • Alpha deposit ≠ realisation. Values for tokens sent to Binance Alpha are marked at the deposit date. Alpha sells inventory to buyers over time, so the price actually realised may differ.
  • Tokenomics source. The allocation mapping rests on published third-party tokenomics pages that agree with each other, not on a signed document from the project.
