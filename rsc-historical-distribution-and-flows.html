Three findings frame everything below.

The distribution is small relative to the supply, and concentrated. In six years the ResearchHub complex has released 235.1M RSC — 23.5% of the supply — and 87.0% of the total still sits in wallets it controls. Of what was released, 46.5M went to 21 large one-off allocations, 35.4M to 39 addresses on a recurring monthly schedule, 42.5M to 1,347 one-off grantees, and only 9.7M to the 651 addresses that look like ordinary platform reward recipients.

Direct selling by ResearchHub wallets is modest in dollars and heavily concentrated in 2026. 23,916,405 RSC has moved from a complex wallet to an exchange or a pool across the token's life, worth $3,069,912 at the prices that prevailed on those days. Two thirds of the quantity — 15.6M RSC — moved in 2026 alone, but because the price had already fallen it raised only $1.44M. The complex bought 8.06M RSC back for $2.21M over the same span, so net disposal is 15.9M RSC and $860,522.

The people who received RSC sold most of it, and the ones paid a salary sold the most. The 39 recurring-compensation wallets received 35.4M RSC and have sent 22.0M to trading venues, realising $4,962,841 — more than double what the complex itself realised. The 1,347 one-off grantees sold 22.6M for $4,205,317. Across every cohort, only 100.7M RSC of the 235.1M distributed is still held by the address that received it.

One attribution this analysis could not support

The wallet that took 70,000,000 RSC directly from the genesis mint, 0x1288d4d0…8D34, is sometimes assumed to belong to Brian Armstrong, who co-founded ResearchHub and is CEO of Coinbase. Nothing on-chain supports that. The address has no ENS record and no explorer name tag. barmstrong.eth resolves to 0x5b76f5b8…a1f0, which has received a lifetime total of 1 RSC across twelve dust transfers from a single spam address and holds that 1 RSC today; it has never transacted with 0x1288d4d0…8D34 or with any ResearchHub wallet. The 70M wallet is treated in this document as an unnamed genesis recipient, which is what the evidence shows.

2. Method, sources and definitions

How the ledger was built

Transfers. Every ERC-20 Transfer log for RSC on Ethereum from the deployment block 10,570,723 (1 August 2020) and on Base from block 12,647,115 (2 April 2024), through 13 August 2026 — 1,379,897 events, with no unrecovered block ranges on either chain. 30,233 of those are zero-value transfers from address-poisoning activity and are excluded from every figure.

Time. Base runs an exact two-second cadence — verified at zero drift against six anchors spanning its entire life — so block times there are arithmetic. Ethereum block times are interpolated between 7,590 sampled blocks, two thousand blocks apart.

Price. A single daily RSC/USD series covering 2,129 calendar days from 17 October 2020. Listed days use the venue close, preferring Coinbase, then Gate.io, then MEXC. Days before the first exchange listing use the median RSC/WETH rate implied by that day's Uniswap swaps on Ethereum, multiplied by the ETH/USD close. Over the 622 days where both are available the two methods differ by a median of 2.6%. The series independently reproduces CoinGecko's all-time high — it peaks at $1.49 on 3 January 2025 against CoinGecko's $1.51 on 4 January — and its 2022 low of $0.0037 matches CoinGecko's $0.0031 all-time low.

TermDefinition used throughout
The complex26 addresses: the three treasuries, the Foundation Community Safe, the hot wallet, the three genesis recipients that still act as company wallets, the vesting escrows, the distributor and hop wallets, the three exchange deposit funnels, and the staking vault. Listed in full in §11.
A saleRSC arriving at a labelled centralised-exchange wallet, at an AMM pool, or at a DEX router or settlement contract. Per the brief, every transfer to an exchange is counted as a sale, valued at that day's price. This is an upper bound: a deposit is not proof of execution.
A purchaseRSC leaving one of those venues into a wallet that is not itself a venue. Valued the same way. Also an upper bound — an exchange withdrawal is indistinguishable on-chain from the return of coins deposited earlier.
CohortAssigned mechanically from each address's funding history, not by judgement. The rules are in §4.

The recipients realised four times what the company did

ResearchHub's own wallets sold 23.9M RSC for $3.07M across the token's life. The people and entities it paid sold 64.7M RSC for $13.07M over the same period — and the two cohorts closest to the organisation, recurring compensation and one-off grantees, account for $9.17M of that on their own.

Almost all of it went through pools and DEX aggregators rather than exchange deposits: 62.8M of the 64.7M. That is what the on-chain sell-side of this token has consisted of.

The same cohorts also bought 11.6M RSC back for $3.40M, so their net realisation is 53.1M RSC and $9.67M. The recurring-compensation cohort is the most active on both sides — 22.0M sold and 8.9M bought — which is the profile of wallets being actively traded, not simply drained.

Two distinct patterns, split almost exactly at the start of 2023

The early cohort sold directly, and sold cheaply. The nine wallets first funded before 2023 received 17,968,686 RSC worth $554,016 at the time, and sold 8,935,497 of it for $537,753 — realising almost the whole grant value into a market where RSC traded between a tenth of a cent and four cents.

The later cohort barely touches a venue directly. The twelve wallets first funded from 2023 onward received 28,507,177 RSC worth $13,601,537 — 96% of the cohort's dollar value — and show only 737,177 RSC of transfers to any trading venue. Yet most of them hold nothing today. The RSC left by onward transfer to another wallet, and the disposal happens one hop further out.

The clearest case is the largest allocation ever made: 10,000,000 RSC to 0xe4758f76…aa7f on 24 November 2024, three weeks before the all-time high, worth $6,849,883 at the time. That wallet holds nothing. It sent 5,749,990 to 0xd8d6ffe3…3cef0 and 4,000,010 to 0xecd3b4b7…d2b9 — and 0xecd3b4b7…d2b9 exists for one purpose: it has received 10,340,428 RSC from those two addresses and a third, and forwarded every one of them to Gate.io across 71 transfers, retaining nothing.

Not every large allocation is external. 0xdfb1fb21…1d58's 6,718,750 RSC went straight back to Treasury 2 and a treasury signer — an internal round trip that the mechanical rule cannot distinguish from a grant, and that should not be read as a distribution to a third party.

Gross figures on this scale are dominated by arbitrage: the same RSC is bought out of one pool and sold into another many times over, so 770M of "buying" against a 1B supply reflects turnover, not accumulation. The net is the meaningful number, and over six years it is −29.7M RSC and −$4.74M — the market as a whole has been a net seller by a small margin, which is the arithmetic counterpart of the complex and its recipients being net distributors.

No single purchase in the token's history exceeds $170,000. The three 2026 entries are larger in RSC terms and far smaller in dollars, which is the same pattern the complex's own selling shows: quantity has moved to 2026, value has not.

What the listings durably changed is where trading happens. In 2024 the on-chain pools were the market. By mid-2026 the four exchanges carry roughly two thirds of turnover and the pools one third, and the total has settled at $8–18M a month against a $13M market capitalisation.

10. What this document does not establish

  • A deposit to an exchange is not a proven sale. The brief asks that every transfer to an exchange be counted as one, and that convention is applied throughout, but on-chain visibility ends at the exchange wallet. Some of the 23.9M attributed to the complex may have been collateral, market-making inventory or a transfer between the project's own exchange accounts.
  • An exchange withdrawal is not a proven purchase. The same limit applies in reverse, which is why buy-side figures are described as upper bounds.
  • Cohorts are behavioural, not contractual. "Recurring compensation" identifies a monthly payment cadence. It cannot distinguish an employee from a contractor, a grant programme or a retained advisor, and no wallet in this document is attributed to a named person.
  • The 70M genesis wallet is unattributed. There is no on-chain, ENS or public-label evidence connecting 0x1288d4d0…8D34 to Brian Armstrong or to any other individual, and barmstrong.eth has never held a meaningful RSC balance. Any such attribution would have to come from a source outside the chain.
  • Pre-listing prices are derived, not quoted. For the 1,015 days before the first exchange listing, price comes from the median RSC/WETH rate implied by that day's Uniswap swaps. On thin days that rate can be wide; the median absolute divergence against Gate.io over the 622 overlapping days is 2.6%, and the p90 is 7.7%.
  • Onward transfers are not traced past the first hop in the cohort tables. Where a wallet passes RSC to another non-venue wallet that then sells, §5 shows the first wallet as "not sold". The largest such case is set out explicitly in §5.1, but there will be others below the reporting threshold.
  • Gross venue flow is inflated by arbitrage and is reported only as a net figure in §7 for that reason.
